# The Aorist Indicative Passive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, δείκνυμι)*

---

## 1. What the Aorist Indicative Passive Is

This is the form for "**it was given**", "**it was placed**" — a single completed action in the past, with the subject undergoing it rather than performing it.

The aorist passive is where μι-verbs are at their easiest. Whatever they do elsewhere, here they take the same ‑θη‑ marker as every other Greek verb and the same endings:

| Verb | Aorist passive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι | **ἐδόθην** | I was given |
| τίθημι | **ἐτέθην** | I was placed |
| ἵστημι | **ἐστάθην** | I was set up, I was made to stand |
| δείκνυμι | **ἐδείχθην** | I was shown |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**augment + short-vowel stem + ‑θη‑ + secondary active endings**

The endings are *active* in shape, not middle — this is the single most surprising fact about the aorist passive, and it holds for ω-verbs and μι-verbs alike.

### 2.2 Full Paradigm: **δίδωμι** → **ἐδόθην** "I was given"

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **ἐδόθην** | I was given |
| 2 sg | **ἐδόθης** | you were given |
| 3 sg | **ἐδόθη** | he/she/it was given |
| 1 pl | **ἐδόθημεν** | we were given |
| 2 pl | **ἐδόθητε** | you (pl.) were given |
| 3 pl | **ἐδόθησαν** | they were given |

The same endings on the other stems: ἐτέθην, ἐτέθης, ἐτέθη…; ἐστάθην, ἐστάθης, ἐστάθη…

### 2.3 Beyond the Indicative

Built on the same ‑θε‑/‑θη‑ stem, and worth recognizing:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | **δοθῆναι** | to be given |
| Participle | **δοθείς, δοθεῖσα, δοθέν** | having been given |
| Subjunctive | **δοθῇ** | (that) it be given |
| Imperative | **δόθητι** | be given! |

### 2.4 τίθημι and the Aspirates

τίθημι's passive is **ἐτέθην**, not *ἐθέθην. Greek does not tolerate two aspirated consonants (θ…θ) in successive syllables, so the first dissimilates to τ. The same rule explains θρίξ / τριχός and τρέφω / θρέψω.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Active endings, passive meaning** | ἐδόθην, ἐδόθης, ἐδόθη look like the endings of a verb such as ἔστην. Do not expect ‑μην, ‑σο, ‑το here. |
| **2. Short stem vowel before ‑θη‑** | δο‑, θε‑, στα‑: ἐδόθην, ἐτέθην, ἐστάθην. The long vowel of δίδωμι, τίθημι does not appear. |
| **3. ἐστάθην vs. ἔστην** | ἵστημι has both: **ἐστάθην** "I was set up" (passive, someone else did it) and **ἔστην** "I stood" (intransitive root aorist, active in form and meaning). The difference matters constantly in narrative. |
| **4. No middle here** | The aorist middle is a separate form (ἐδόμην "I gave for myself"). Unlike the present, the aorist keeps middle and passive fully apart. |
| **5. The agent is ὑπό + genitive** | ἐδόθη ὑπὸ τοῦ βασιλέως "it was given by the king"; an instrument takes the plain dative. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἡ χώρα τοῖς συμμάχοις ἐδόθη.** — "The land was given to the allies."
- **οἱ νόμοι ὑπὸ Σόλωνος ἐτέθησαν.** — "The laws were laid down by Solon."
- **τὸ τρόπαιον ἐστάθη ἐν τῷ πεδίῳ.** — "The trophy was set up on the plain."
- **ταῦτα πᾶσιν ἐδείχθη σαφῶς.** — "This was clearly shown to everyone."
- **ἀφέθησαν οἱ αἰχμάλωτοι ἄνευ λύτρων.** — "The prisoners were released without ransom."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Documents and decrees.** ἐδόθη, ἐτέθη and ἐψηφίσθη are the language of inscriptions and of Thucydides' treaty texts: the actor is often deliberately left unnamed.
2. **The stem is worth banking.** Learn δοθ‑, τεθ‑, σταθ‑ once and the infinitive, participle, subjunctive and future passive (δοθήσομαι) all follow from it.
3. **Reading tip.** Augment + short stem + θη is unmistakable. If you also see ‑σαν at the end, it is third plural: ἐδόθησαν.
