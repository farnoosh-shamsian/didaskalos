# The Perfect Indicative Middle/Passive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, δύναμαι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

A completed action **whose result still stands**, with the subject either acting for itself or undergoing the action: δέδοται "it has been given (and is theirs now)", ἕσταμαι "I stand".

| Verb | Perfect middle/passive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι | **δέδομαι** | I have been given / I have given of my own |
| τίθημι | **τέθειμαι** | I have been placed / I have set for myself |
| ἵστημι | **ἕσταμαι** | I stand |
| δύναμαι | **δεδύνημαι** | I have been able |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**reduplication + short stem + middle endings**, attached directly with no thematic vowel.

### 2.2 Paradigm: **δίδωμι** → **δέδομαι**

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **δέδομαι** | I have been given |
| 2 sg | **δέδοσαι** | you have been given |
| 3 sg | **δέδοται** | it has been given |
| 1 pl | **δεδόμεθα** | we have been given |
| 2 pl | **δέδοσθε** | you (pl.) have been given |
| 3 pl | **δέδονται** | they have been given |

τίθημι: τέθειμαι, τέθεισαι, τέθειται… ἵστημι: ἕσταμαι, ἕστασαι, ἕσταται…

### 2.3 Consonant Stems

δείκνυμι has a consonant stem, so the endings assimilate and the third plural must be periphrastic:

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **δέδειγμαι** |
| 2 sg | **δέδειξαι** |
| 3 sg | **δέδεικται** |
| 3 pl | **δεδειγμένοι εἰσί(ν)** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Endings attach directly** | δέδο‑ται, not *δεδό‑ε‑ται. The linking vowel of the thematic verbs is absent throughout. |
| **2. 2 sg keeps its σ** | δέδοσαι, not *δέδου. Compare ω-verbs, where λέλυσαι also keeps it after the stem vowel. |
| **3. δέδοται is a favourite of documents** | "It has been granted" — the standard perfect of decrees and grants, alongside ἔδοξεν. |
| **4. ἕσταμαι = "I stand"** | ἵστημι's perfect is intransitive whichever voice it wears; the active ἕστηκα is commoner in Attic prose. |
| **5. Perfect vs. present** | δέδοται (perfect, result stands) vs. δίδοται (present, in the act of being given). The reduplication vowel differs: δε‑ vs. δι‑. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἡ χώρα τοῖς συμμάχοις δέδοται.** — "The land has been given to the allies."
- **οἱ νόμοι καλῶς τέθεινται.** — "The laws have been well established."
- **ἕσταται τὸ τρόπαιον ἐν τῷ πεδίῳ.** — "The trophy stands on the plain."
- **οὐ δεδύνηται ταῦτα ποιῆσαι.** — "He has not been able to do this."
- **δέδεικται σαφῶς ὁ λόγος.** — "The argument has been clearly demonstrated."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Formal and legal language.** δέδοται, τέθειται and δέδεικται carry the weight of "it stands established" — hence their frequency in decrees and in philosophical proof.
2. **δέδεικται in argument.** Plato and Aristotle close a demonstration with it: "it has been shown".
3. **Reading tip.** Reduplication with ε plus a middle ending attached to a short stem: perfect middle/passive. Add an augment and it becomes the pluperfect ἐδέδοτο.
