# The Perfect Imperative Middle/Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, μιμνῄσκομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Imperative Is

A command that a **state be in force**: not "do it" but "let it stand done". λελύσθω is "let it be released once and for all"; γεγράφθω is "let it stand written".

| Imperative | Force |
| --- | --- |
| Present λύου | keep being freed |
| Aorist λύθητι | be freed (once) |
| **Perfect λέλυσο** | **be in the state of having been freed** |

This is a rare form, but it has one very common member: **μέμνησο** "remember".

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**reduplicated perfect middle/passive stem + imperative endings**, with no thematic vowel and no augment:

| Person | Ending | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | ‑σο | **λέλυσο** | be freed! (and stay so) |
| 3 sg | ‑σθω | **λελύσθω** | let it stand released |
| 2 pl | ‑σθε | **λέλυσθε** | be freed! (pl.) |
| 3 pl | ‑σθων | **λελύσθων** | let them stand released |

### 2.2 Consonant Stems

When the stem ends in a consonant the ending assimilates, and the results are the forms you will actually meet:

| Verb | 3 sg imperative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| γράφω | **γεγράφθω** | let it stand written |
| λέγω / εἴρηκα | **εἰρήσθω** | let it have been said |
| τάσσω | **τετάχθω** | let him be posted |
| μιμνῄσκομαι | **μέμνησο** | remember! |

### 2.3 εἰρήσθω and the Argument

εἰρήσθω is a fixed formula in philosophy and oratory: "let that be taken as said", closing a topic. Plato and Aristotle both use it to move on.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Reduplication, no augment** | λέ‑λυσο. An augment would make it the pluperfect indicative ἐλέλυσο. |
| **2. μέμνησο is a present in force** | μέμνημαι means "I remember", so its imperative means "remember", not "have remembered". |
| **3. The third person is the usual one** | γεγράφθω, εἰρήσθω, τετάχθω: the perfect imperative is mostly used impersonally, to declare a state settled. |
| **4. Periphrasis is also available** | λελυμένος ἔστω "let him be in a freed state" says the same thing with the participle and the imperative of εἰμί. |
| **5. Rare in narrative, common in argument** | You will meet it in philosophical prose and decrees far more than in history. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **μέμνησο τῶν φίλων.** — "Remember your friends."
- **ταῦτα μὲν οὖν εἰρήσθω περὶ τούτων.** — "Let that be enough said on this subject."
- **γεγράφθω ὁ νόμος ἐν στήλῃ.** — "Let the law stand inscribed on a stele."
- **τετάχθω ἕκαστος ᾗ ἐτάχθη.** — "Let each man stay posted where he was posted."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Closing formulas.** εἰρήσθω and its relatives end a section of an argument — the Greek equivalent of "so much for that".
2. **Legal permanence.** In decrees the perfect imperative expresses that something is to remain in force, not merely to happen.
3. **Reading tip.** Reduplication plus ‑σθω is unmistakable; if the stem ends in a consonant, expect assimilation (‑φθω, ‑χθω, ‑σθω).
