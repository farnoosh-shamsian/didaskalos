# The Pluperfect Indicative Active (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, ποιέω)*

---

## 1. What the Pluperfect Indicative Active Is

The perfect says "I have loosed — and the result stands **now**." The pluperfect moves that whole picture into the past: "I **had** loosed — and the result stood **then**."

| Tense | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aorist | ἔλυσα | "I loosed" (the event) |
| Perfect | λέλυκα | "I have loosed" (result stands now) |
| **Pluperfect** | **ἐλελύκη** | "I had loosed" (result stood at some past moment) |

It is the rarest of the Greek tenses: a narrative normally uses the aorist and lets the sequence speak for itself, reaching for the pluperfect only when the earlier action's *result* matters to the moment being described.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**augment ἐ‑ + reduplicated perfect stem + ‑κ‑ + secondary endings**

The pluperfect is the perfect stem with a past-tense wrapper: the augment in front, secondary endings behind.

### 2.2 Full Paradigm: **λύω** → **ἐλελύκη** "I had loosed"

| Person | Form | Later Attic variant | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **ἐλελύκη** | ἐλελύκειν | I had loosed |
| 2 sg | **ἐλελύκης** | ἐλελύκεις | you had loosed |
| 3 sg | **ἐλελύκει(ν)** | — | he/she/it had loosed |
| 1 pl | **ἐλελύκεμεν** | — | we had loosed |
| 2 pl | **ἐλελύκετε** | — | you (pl.) had loosed |
| 3 pl | **ἐλελύκεσαν** | — | they had loosed |

Both singular sets are correct Attic; the ‑ειν, ‑εις forms grow commoner over the fourth century and are standard later.

### 2.3 The Second Pluperfect (no κ)

Verbs with a second (κ-less) perfect build the pluperfect on that stem:

| Perfect | Pluperfect | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| γέγραφα | **ἐγεγράφη** | I had written |
| πέπονθα | **ἐπεπόνθη** | I had suffered |
| λέλοιπα | **ἐλελοίπη** | I had left |

### 2.4 When the Augment Is Invisible

If the perfect reduplication already begins with a vowel — because the verb does — the augment merges into that long vowel and nothing extra appears:

- ἀγγέλλω → perfect ἤγγελκα → pluperfect **ἠγγέλκη**
- ἀκούω → perfect ἀκήκοα → pluperfect **ἠκηκόη**

---

## 3. Key Rules and Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Augment the reduplication, don't replace it** | ἐ‑ goes *in front of* λελυκ‑: ἐλελύκη. Never drop the reduplication to make room for the augment. |
| **2. The endings are not the imperfect's** | Imperfect ἔλυον, ἔλυες; pluperfect ἐλελύκη, ἐλελύκης. The ‑η/‑ης/‑ει set belongs to this tense alone. |
| **3. 3 sg looks like 1 sg of the ‑ειν set** | ἐλελύκει can be 3 sg; ἐλελύκειν is 1 sg. The ν is doing real work. |
| **4. ᾔδη is a pluperfect with imperfect meaning** | From οἶδα "I know": ᾔδη / ᾔδειν means simply "I knew", not "I had known". Its stablemates ἐῴκη (ἔοικα) behave the same way. |
| **5. Only the indicative exists** | There is no pluperfect subjunctive, optative, imperative or participle in ordinary use — where a subordinate clause needs the sense, the perfect stem supplies it. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἐπεὶ δὲ ταῦτα ἐπεποιήκεσαν, ἀπῆλθον.** — "When they had done this, they departed."
- **οὐκ ᾔδη ὅ τι λέγοι.** — "I did not know what he meant."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Subordinate clauses of time** — after ἐπεί, ἐπειδή, πρίν — are the pluperfect's natural home, where it fixes one action as prior to another past action.
2. **The aorist often does the job instead.** Greek is happy to narrate with successive aorists and leave the sequence to context; do not expect a pluperfect at every point where one event precedes another.
3. **Reading tip.** The signature is augment *plus* reduplication in one word: ἐ‑λε‑λύκ‑η. Spot both and you have the tense before you reach the ending.
