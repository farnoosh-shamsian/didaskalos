# The Aorist Optative Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, πέμπω)*

---

## 1. What the Aorist Optative Passive Is

The optative is the mood of the wished-for and the hypothetical. In the passive it says "**may it be freed**", "if it **should be** freed", or — most often in prose — it reports in a past context what was said or feared in the present.

The aorist marks the action as a single complete event; it carries no past time of its own.

| Mood | Form | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subjunctive | λυθῇ | that it be freed (vivid, primary sequence) |
| **Optative** | **λυθείη** | that it might be freed (past sequence, wish, potential) |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**aorist passive stem in ‑θε‑ + ‑ίη‑/‑εί‑ + secondary active endings.** No augment: the optative never takes one.

### 2.2 Full Paradigm: **λύω** → **λυθείην** "may I be freed"

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λυθείην** | may I be freed |
| 2 sg | **λυθείης** | may you be freed |
| 3 sg | **λυθείη** | may he/she/it be freed |
| 1 pl | **λυθεῖμεν** (λυθείημεν) | may we be freed |
| 2 pl | **λυθεῖτε** (λυθείητε) | may you be freed |
| 3 pl | **λυθεῖεν** (λυθείησαν) | may they be freed |

The longer plural forms in ‑ίη‑ are the older set; Attic prefers the shorter λυθεῖμεν, λυθεῖτε, λυθεῖεν.

### 2.3 Other Verbs

| Verb | Aorist passive | Optative 3 sg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| γράφω | ἐγράφην | **γραφείη** |
| πέμπω | ἐπέμφθην | **πεμφθείη** |
| ἄγω | ἤχθην | **ἀχθείη** |
| ποιέω | ἐποιήθην | **ποιηθείη** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. ‑θει‑ plus ‑η‑ is the signature** | λυ‑**θει**‑**η**‑ν: the θ marks the passive, the ει/ιη marks the optative. |
| **2. No augment** | ἐλύθην is indicative, λυθείην optative. |
| **3. Optative in secondary sequence** | After a past main verb the subjunctive of direct speech becomes optative: εἶπεν ἵνα λυθείη "he said so that it might be released" (direct: ἵνα λυθῇ). |
| **4. Potential optative takes ἄν** | λυθείη ἄν "it might be freed". Without ἄν the same form is a wish. |
| **5. Distinguish from the participle** | λυθείς, λυθέντος is the participle; λυθείη, λυθεῖεν the optative. Both contain ‑θει‑, but only the participle takes case endings. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **εἴθε λυθείησαν οἱ αἰχμάλωτοι.** — "If only the prisoners might be freed!"
- **ἐφοβεῖτο μὴ ἡ ἐπιστολὴ ἀναγνωσθείη.** — "He was afraid the letter might be read out."
- **ἔπεμψαν ἀγγέλους ὅπως σπονδαὶ γένοιντο καὶ ἡ εἰρήνη κυρωθείη.** — "They sent messengers so that a truce might be made and the peace ratified."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Secondary sequence** is the commonest home of this form: a purpose or fear clause depending on a past verb.
2. **Wishes** are introduced by εἴθε or εἰ γάρ: εἴθε γραφείη "would that it were written".
3. **Reading tip.** The optative is the rarest of the moods in later Greek but thrives in classical Attic prose — in Plato and Xenophon it is everywhere, and ‑θείη is its passive shape.
