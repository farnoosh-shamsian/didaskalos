# The Aorist Optative Middle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵημι)*

---

## 1. What This Optative Is

A wish, a possibility, or a reported intention in a past context, with the action seen as **one complete act**: δοῖτο "may he give", εἴθε συνθοίμεθα "if only we might come to terms".

Built on the root aorist stem, these forms are short and their diphthong is unmistakable.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigms

| Person | δίδωμι | τίθημι | ἵημι (ἀφ‑) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **δοίμην** | **θείμην** | **ἀφείμην** |
| 2 sg | **δοῖο** | **θεῖο** | **ἀφεῖο** |
| 3 sg | **δοῖτο** | **θεῖτο** | **ἀφεῖτο** |
| 1 pl | **δοίμεθα** | **θείμεθα** | **ἀφείμεθα** |
| 2 pl | **δοῖσθε** | **θεῖσθε** | **ἀφεῖσθε** |
| 3 pl | **δοῖντο** | **θεῖντο** | **ἀφεῖντο** |

ἵστημι, whose middle aorist is sigmatic, gives στησαίμην, στήσαιο, στήσαιτο.

### 2.2 Formation

**short stem + optative ‑ι‑ + secondary middle endings.** The ι fuses with the stem vowel: δο + ι → δοι, θε + ι → θει.

### 2.3 Present or Aorist?

| Tense | 3 sg | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | **διδοῖτο** | that it be given (ongoing) |
| **Aorist** | **δοῖτο** | that it be given (once) |

Only the reduplication δι‑ separates them.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐδόμην is indicative; δοίμην is optative. |
| **2. The diphthong marks the mood** | οι in δοῖτο, ει in θεῖτο. Compare the subjunctive δῶται, θῆται, which has a long vowel instead. |
| **3. Potential with ἄν** | δοῖτο ἄν "he might give". Without ἄν it is a wish, usually with εἴθε. |
| **4. Secondary sequence** | After a past main verb the subjunctive of direct speech becomes this optative: ἐβούλετο ἵνα ἀποδοῖτο. |
| **5. Compounds keep the accent off the preposition's own syllable where the rules require** | ἀποδοῖτο, ἐπιθεῖτο, προσθεῖτο. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **εἴθε τὴν χώραν μὴ ἀποδοῖντο.** — "If only they would not sell the land!"
- **οὐκ ἂν ἐπιθεῖτο νυκτὸς οὕτω μικρᾷ δυνάμει.** — "He would not attack by night with so small a force."
- **ἐβουλεύοντο ὅπως συνθεῖντο πρὸς τοὺς πολεμίους.** — "They were deliberating how they might come to terms with the enemy."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Attic prose in past narrative** is where the optative lives: purpose and fear clauses depending on a past verb.
2. **The potential optative with ἄν** is a politeness as much as a probability — "one might say", "he would not do that".
3. **Reading tip.** Short stem, οι or ει, and a middle ending: aorist optative middle. Add the reduplication and you are in the present.
