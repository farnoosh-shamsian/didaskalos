# The Aorist Middle Participle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵημι)*

---

## 1. What This Participle Is

The aorist middle participle presents a single completed act performed **by the subject, for the subject**: θέμενος τὰ ὅπλα "having grounded his arms", δόμενος "having given of his own".

μι-verbs form their aorist middle from a **root aorist** — the bare stem with no ‑σα‑ — so the participle is short and easy to overlook:

| Verb | Aorist middle indicative | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι "give" | ἐδόμην | **δόμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| τίθημι "place" | ἐθέμην | **θέμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| ἵημι "send" | (ἀφ)είμην | **(ἀφ)έμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |

ἵστημι is the exception: its middle is the sigmatic **ἐστησάμην**, participle **στησάμενος** "having set up for oneself".

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Declension: **θέμενος** "having placed (for oneself)"

Like every ‑μενος participle it declines as a first/second declension adjective:

| Case | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Neut. sg. | Masc. pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | θέμενος | θεμένη | θέμενον | θέμενοι |
| Gen. | θεμένου | θεμένης | θεμένου | θεμένων |
| Dat. | θεμένῳ | θεμένῃ | θεμένῳ | θεμένοις |
| Acc. | θέμενον | θεμένην | θέμενον | θεμένους |

δόμενος and ἕμενος follow exactly the same pattern.

### 2.2 Compounds Are the Normal Case

In real texts the simple forms are rarer than their compounds, and the preposition is where the meaning lives:

| Compound | Participle | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ἀποδίδωμι | **ἀποδόμενος** | having sold (given away for oneself) |
| ἐπιτίθημι | **ἐπιθέμενος** | having attacked (set upon) |
| προτίθημι | **προθέμενος** | having proposed, having set before oneself |
| ἀφίημι | **ἀφέμενος** | having let go, having given up |
| συντίθημι | **συνθέμενος** | having agreed, having made a compact |

### 2.3 Accent

The accent falls on the **penult** of the participle and stays there: θέμενος, θεμένου, θεμένων. In compounds it cannot retreat past the preposition: **ἐπιθέμενος**, not *ἐπίθεμενος.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment, no ‑σα‑** | Indicative ἐθέμην has the augment; the participle θέμενος has neither augment nor sigma. What is left is the bare root plus ‑μενος. |
| **2. Aorist θέμενος vs. present τιθέμενος** | The present participle keeps the reduplicating τι‑: τιθέμενος "placing". Drop those two letters and you have the aorist. This is the single commonest confusion. |
| **3. Middle, not passive** | The passive is τεθείς "having been placed", a third-declension form. ‑μενος here means the subject acted for itself. |
| **4. συνθέμενος is a technical term** | "Having made an agreement" — it is the standard word in treaties and contracts, and σύνθημα "watchword, compact" comes from the same root. |
| **5. Prior in time, usually** | Like all aorist participles it normally reports an act completed before the main verb: δόμενος ἀπῆλθεν "he gave it and left". |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **θέμενοι τὰ ὅπλα ἀνεπαύοντο.** — "Having grounded their arms, they rested."
- **ἀποδόμενος τὴν οἰκίαν ἀπῆλθεν ἐκ τῆς πόλεως.** — "Having sold his house, he left the city."
- **ἐπιθέμενοι νυκτὸς τοὺς φύλακας ἐκράτησαν.** — "Attacking by night, they overpowered the guards."
- **συνθέμενοι πρὸς ἀλλήλους εἰρήνην ἦγον.** — "Having made a compact with one another, they kept the peace."
- **προθέμενος τὸν λόγον ἤρξατο λέγειν.** — "Having set out his theme, he began to speak."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Historians live on these forms.** Thucydides and Xenophon narrate campaigns with strings of aorist participles — θέμενοι, ἐπιθέμενοι, ἀποδόμενοι — where a less participle-friendly language would need a string of finite verbs joined by "and".
2. **The genitive absolute.** With a genitive noun outside the main clause: ἐπιθεμένων τῶν πολεμίων "when the enemy attacked".
3. **Learn them with their prepositions.** δόμενος and θέμενος on their own are much less frequent than ἀποδόμενος, ἐπιθέμενος and συνθέμενος; the compound is the word you will actually meet.
