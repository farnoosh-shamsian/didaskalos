# The Aorist Imperative Middle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵημι)*

---

## 1. What This Imperative Is

A command to do something once, for oneself: δοῦ "give of your own", θοῦ "set for yourself", ἀπόδου "sell".

These are root-aorist forms, so they are among the shortest imperatives in Greek — and in compounds the accent jumps back onto the preposition, which is how you recognize them.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigms

| Person | δίδωμι | τίθημι | ἵημι (ἀφ‑) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **δοῦ** | **θοῦ** | **ἀφοῦ** |
| 3 sg | **δόσθω** | **θέσθω** | **ἀφέσθω** |
| 2 pl | **δόσθε** | **θέσθε** | **ἄφεσθε** |
| 3 pl | **δόσθων** | **θέσθων** | **ἀφέσθων** |

The 2 sg δοῦ and θοῦ come from δό‑σο and θέ‑σο: the σ dropped between vowels and the vowels contracted.

ἵστημι, with its sigmatic middle aorist, gives στῆσαι, στησάσθω, στήσασθε.

### 2.2 Compounds and the Accent

| Compound | 2 sg | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ἀποδίδωμι | **ἀπόδου** | sell! |
| ἐπιτίθημι | **ἐπίθου** | attack! set upon! |
| προστίθημι | **πρόσθου** | add for yourself! |
| συντίθημι | **σύνθου** | agree! |
| ἀφίημι | **ἄφου** | let go! |

The simple forms take a circumflex on the ending (δοῦ); the compounds throw the accent back (ἀπόδου).

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐδόμην is indicative; δοῦ is the imperative. |
| **2. δοῦ vs. δίδοσο** | δοῦ (aorist) commands a single act; δίδοσο (present) commands an ongoing practice. |
| **3. δός is active, δοῦ is middle** | One letter apart in sense: "give it" vs. "give of your own / sell". |
| **4. Prohibitions** | μή + aorist subjunctive: μὴ ἀποδῷ "don't sell", never a negative imperative. |
| **5. Accent tells you it is a compound** | ἀπόδου, ἐπίθου, σύνθου: the retraction is the visible sign of the preposition. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἀπόδου τὴν οἰκίαν.** — "Sell the house."
- **θοῦ τὰ ὅπλα χαμαί.** — "Put your arms on the ground."
- **σύνθου πρὸς αὐτοὺς εἰρήνην.** — "Make peace with them."
- **ἐπίθεσθε τοῖς πολεμίοις.** — "Attack the enemy." (pl.)
- **δόσθω ἕκαστος τὸ μέρος.** — "Let each give his share."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Commerce and diplomacy.** ἀπόδου and σύνθου are the everyday imperatives of selling and treaty-making.
2. **Battle orders.** ἐπίθεσθε is a standard command in the historians.
3. **Reading tip.** A one-syllable ending ‑οῦ, or ‑σθω/‑σθε on a bare short stem, with the accent thrown back if a preposition is attached: the μι-verb aorist middle imperative.
