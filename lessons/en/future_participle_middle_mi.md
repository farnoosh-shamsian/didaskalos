# The Future Middle Participle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What This Participle Is

"About to give", "going to set up" — an action still to come, expressed as an adjective agreeing with a noun. Its commonest job by far is to state **purpose**.

| Verb | Future middle | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι "give" | δώσομαι | **δωσόμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| τίθημι "place" | θήσομαι | **θησόμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| ἵστημι "set up" | στήσομαι | **στησόμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| ἵημι "send" | ἥσομαι | **ἡσόμενος, ‑η, ‑ον** |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**long stem vowel + ‑σ‑ + ‑όμενος, ‑ομένη, ‑όμενον**, declined like a first/second declension adjective. No augment: the future never has one.

| Case | Masc. | Fem. | Neut. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. sg. | δωσόμενος | δωσομένη | δωσόμενον |
| Gen. sg. | δωσομένου | δωσομένης | δωσομένου |
| Dat. sg. | δωσομένῳ | δωσομένῃ | δωσομένῳ |
| Acc. sg. | δωσόμενον | δωσομένην | δωσόμενον |
| Nom. pl. | δωσόμενοι | δωσόμεναι | δωσόμενα |

### 2.2 Purpose with ὡς

A future participle after a verb of motion states the goal of the journey, and ὡς makes the purpose explicit:

- **ἦλθεν ὡς δωσόμενος τὰ χρήματα.** — "He came in order to give the money."
- **ἔπεμψαν πρέσβεις στησομένους τρόπαιον.** — "They sent envoys to set up a trophy."

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. σ marks the future** | δω‑**σ**‑όμενος. Without it you have the present διδόμενος "being given"; with a reduplication you have the perfect δεδομένος "having been given". Three participles, three stems. |
| **2. Long stem vowel** | δω‑, θη‑, στη‑ throughout the future, unlike the short δο‑, θε‑, στα‑ of the aorist. |
| **3. Purpose is its main use** | Greek expresses purpose with a future participle where another language might need a subordinate clause ("in order to …"); with ὡς it is unmistakable. |
| **4. Middle form, often active sense** | δωσόμενος can mean "about to give (of his own)"; many verbs simply have no future active, and their middle participle translates actively. |
| **5. The accent is fixed** | On the ‑ό‑ of ‑όμενος throughout the declension: δωσόμενος, δωσομένου. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἦλθον ὡς δωσόμενοι δίκην.** — "They came to pay the penalty."
- **ἀπέστειλαν ἄνδρας τὰ ὅπλα θησομένους.** — "They sent men to lay down the arms."
- **ὁ στησόμενος τὸ τρόπαιον οὐ παρῆν.** — "The man who was going to set up the trophy was not present."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Historians** use it constantly with verbs of sending and coming: the participle tells you what the expedition was for.
2. **With the article** it names an intended agent: ὁ δωσόμενος "the one who is to give".
3. **Reading tip.** Long vowel + σ + όμενος and no augment: future middle participle. Look for ὡς just before it.
