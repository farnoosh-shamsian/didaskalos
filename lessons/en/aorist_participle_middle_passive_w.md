# The Aorist Middle/Passive Participle (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γίγνομαι, βουλεύω)*

---

## 1. What This Participle Is

An aorist participle presents its action as a **single completed whole**, normally one that happened *before* the main verb: λυσάμενος τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους ἀπῆλθεν "having ransomed the prisoners, he left."

Unlike the present, where one set of forms serves both voices, the aorist keeps middle and passive **formally apart**:

| Voice | Formation | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Middle | ‑σάμενος, ‑σαμένη, ‑σάμενον | **λυσάμενος** | having freed for oneself, having ransomed |
| Passive | ‑θείς, ‑θεῖσα, ‑θέν | **λυθείς** | having been freed |

A treebank tag of "middle/passive" on an aorist therefore points at one of two things: a **deponent** verb, whose middle form carries active meaning, or a form the annotation leaves undecided. Learn both patterns and read the sentence to choose.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Aorist Middle Participle: **λυσάμενος**

**aorist stem + ‑σα‑ + ‑μενος, ‑μένη, ‑μενον**, declined like a first/second declension adjective. No augment.

| Case | Masc. | Fem. | Neut. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. sg. | λυσάμενος | λυσαμένη | λυσάμενον |
| Gen. sg. | λυσαμένου | λυσαμένης | λυσαμένου |
| Dat. sg. | λυσαμένῳ | λυσαμένῃ | λυσαμένῳ |
| Acc. sg. | λυσάμενον | λυσαμένην | λυσάμενον |
| Nom. pl. | λυσάμενοι | λυσάμεναι | λυσάμενα |
| Gen. pl. | λυσαμένων | λυσαμένων | λυσαμένων |

Thematic aorists drop the ‑σα‑ and use the thematic vowel: λαβόμενος, γενόμενος, ἑλόμενος.

### 2.2 The Aorist Passive Participle: **λυθείς**

**aorist passive stem in ‑θε‑ + ‑ίς, ‑εῖσα, ‑έν**, a third declension participle:

| Case | Masc. | Fem. | Neut. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. sg. | λυθείς | λυθεῖσα | λυθέν |
| Gen. sg. | λυθέντος | λυθείσης | λυθέντος |
| Dat. sg. | λυθέντι | λυθείσῃ | λυθέντι |
| Acc. sg. | λυθέντα | λυθεῖσαν | λυθέν |
| Nom. pl. | λυθέντες | λυθεῖσαι | λυθέντα |
| Dat. pl. | λυθεῖσι(ν) | λυθείσαις | λυθεῖσι(ν) |

### 2.3 Telling Them Apart

| Ending seen | Voice | Declension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ‑μενος, ‑μένη, ‑μενον | middle | 1st/2nd (like ἀγαθός) |
| ‑θείς, ‑θεῖσα, ‑θέν | passive | 3rd (like πᾶς) |

The θ is the single most useful letter here: where you see ‑θε‑, the subject is undergoing the action.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment on a participle** | ἐλυσάμην (indicative) vs. λυσάμενος (participle). If an augment appears, it is not a participle. |
| **2. Aspect first, time second** | The aorist participle is *usually* prior to the main verb, but with verbs of speaking it can be simultaneous: εἶπε γελάσας "he said with a laugh". |
| **3. Deponents look middle, mean active** | γενόμενος "having become" and ἀποκρινάμενος "having answered" take middle endings with active force. |
| **4. Accent** | Middle: fixed on ‑μεν‑ in the aorist, λυσάμενος. Passive: λυθείς has a third-declension stem λυθεντ‑ whose accent follows the noun rules. |
| **5. Distinguish from the perfect** | Perfect middle/passive λελυμένος has a reduplication; aorist λυσάμενος has ‑σα‑. Reduplication means result, ‑σα‑ means event. |

## 4. Example Sentences

- **λυσάμενος τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους ἀπῆλθεν.** — "Having ransomed the prisoners, he departed."
- **γενόμενος στρατηγὸς ἐνίκησεν.** — "Having become general, he won."
- **λυθέντες οἱ αἰχμάλωτοι οἴκαδε ἐπανῆλθον.** — "Freed, the prisoners returned home."
- **ταῦτα βουλευσάμενοι ἐψηφίσαντο.** — "Having deliberated on this, they voted."
- **ἀποκριναμένου δὲ τοῦ βασιλέως, ἀπῆλθον οἱ πρέσβεις.** — "When the king had answered, the envoys left." (genitive absolute)

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **The genitive absolute** is the construction to watch: a participle plus a noun in the genitive, both outside the main clause's syntax — ἀποκριναμένου τοῦ βασιλέως.
2. **Attributive with the article** it names a person by what they did: οἱ λυθέντες "those who were freed", ὁ βουλευσάμενος "the one who planned it".
3. **No single equivalent.** The participle packs a whole clause into one word, so it has to be unpacked differently each time: as "having ‑ed", "after ‑ing", "when he had ‑ed", or simply as a second finite verb in sequence. Choose whatever keeps the sentence readable.
