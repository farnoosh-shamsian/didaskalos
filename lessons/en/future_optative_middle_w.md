# The Future Optative Middle (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γίγνομαι, ἀκούω)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

The future optative is the most restricted form in the Greek verb. It has **one job**: to represent a future indicative of direct speech inside an indirect construction after a past main verb.

- Direct: **λύσεται** "he will ransom"
- Reported after a past verb: **εἶπεν ὅτι λύσοιτο** "he said that he would ransom"

It never expresses a wish, and it is never used in a purpose clause with ἵνα. If you meet one outside indirect discourse, look again.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigm: **λυσοίμην**

**future stem in ‑σ‑ + optative ‑οι‑ + secondary middle endings**, with no augment:

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λυσοίμην** | (that) I would ransom |
| 2 sg | **λύσοιο** | (that) you would ransom |
| 3 sg | **λύσοιτο** | (that) he would ransom |
| 1 pl | **λυσοίμεθα** | (that) we would ransom |
| 2 pl | **λύσοισθε** | (that) you would ransom |
| 3 pl | **λύσοιντο** | (that) they would ransom |

### 2.2 Deponent Futures

Because so many Greek futures are middle in form, most future optatives you meet belong to deponents:

| Verb | Future | Future optative 3 sg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| γίγνομαι | γενήσομαι | **γενήσοιτο** |
| ἀκούω | ἀκούσομαι | **ἀκούσοιτο** |
| λαμβάνω | λήψομαι | **λήψοιτο** |
| ὁράω | ὄψομαι | **ὄψοιτο** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Indirect discourse only** | After ὅτι, ὡς, or in indirect questions, following a past main verb. Nowhere else. |
| **2. σ + οι is the signature** | λυ‑**σ**‑**οι**‑το: the σ says future, the οι says optative. |
| **3. Distinguish from the aorist optative** | λύσαιτο (aorist, ‑σαι‑) vs. λύσοιτο (future, ‑σοι‑). One vowel apart, and the difference is time versus aspect. |
| **4. The future indicative is always an option** | Greek may keep the original λύσεται instead of shifting to λύσοιτο; both are correct after a past verb. |
| **5. No augment, no ἄν** | It is not a potential form; ἄν with the future optative does not occur in classical prose. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **εἶπεν ὅτι τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους λύσοιτο.** — "He said that he would ransom the prisoners."
- **ἠρώτησαν εἰ ἡ πόλις ἁλώσοιτο.** — "They asked whether the city would be captured."
- **ἐνόμιζον ὅτι οὐδὲν κακὸν γενήσοιτο.** — "They thought no harm would come."
- **οὐκ ᾔδει τί πείσοιτο.** — "He did not know what he would suffer."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Historians and orators** use it when reporting what someone said would happen — a hallmark of careful Attic style.
2. **Xenophon** is especially fond of it; in Herodotus and later Greek it grows rarer, and Koine has all but lost it.
3. **Reading tip.** If you see ‑σοι‑ with a middle ending in a ὅτι clause after a past verb, you are looking at a future optative: it reports what was going to happen, viewed from the past narrative rather than from the present.
