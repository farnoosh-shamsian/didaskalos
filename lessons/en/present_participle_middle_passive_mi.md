# The Present Middle/Passive Participle (μι-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Present Participle Middle/Passive Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Present** | Action simultaneous with the main verb, in progress or repeated. |
| **Participle** | A verbal adjective: it agrees with a noun in gender, number, and case. |
| **Middle/Passive** | The subject acts on/for itself, or receives the action; in the present the forms are identical. |
| **(mi)** | This lesson concerns the athematic verbs: δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, and middle‑only verbs such as δύναμαι and κεῖμαι. |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation: Short Stem + **‑μενος, ‑μένη, ‑μενον**

The middle/passive participle suffix **‑μενος** attaches **directly to the short present stem**, with no thematic vowel:

| Verb | Stem | Participle | Meaning |
|------|------|------------|---------|
| τίθημι | τιθε‑ | **τιθέμενος, ‑μένη, ‑μενον** | placing for oneself / being placed |
| δίδωμι | διδο‑ | **διδόμενος** | being given |
| ἵστημι | ἱστα‑ | **ἱστάμενος** | standing; being set up |
| δείκνυμι | δεικνυ‑ | **δεικνύμενος** | being shown |

*Compare the thematic verbs: λυ‑ό‑μενος keeps the thematic ο; τιθέ‑μενος has none.*

### 2.2 Declension

**‑μενος** declines exactly like a first/second‑declension adjective (ἀγαθός):

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|
| Nom. sg. | τιθέμενος | τιθεμένη | τιθέμενον |
| Gen. sg. | τιθεμένου | τιθεμένης | τιθεμένου |
| Dat. sg. | τιθεμένῳ | τιθεμένῃ | τιθεμένῳ |
| Acc. sg. | τιθέμενον | τιθεμένην | τιθέμενον |
| Nom. pl. | τιθέμενοι | τιθέμεναι | τιθέμενα |

### 2.3 Athematic Deponent Participles

The middle‑only verbs form the same participle, translated **actively**:

| Verb | Participle | Meaning |
|------|------------|---------|
| **δύναμαι** | **δυνάμενος** | being able |
| **ἐπίσταμαι** | **ἐπιστάμενος** | knowing |
| **κεῖμαι** | **κείμενος** | lying, situated |
| **κάθημαι** | **καθήμενος** | sitting |

---

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. No thematic vowel** | ‑μενος attaches straight to the stem. | διδόμενος, not *διδοόμενος |
| **2. Accent on the stem syllable** | Unlike thematic ‑όμενος, the accent falls before ‑μενος. | τιθέμενος, δυνάμενος |
| **3. Declines like ἀγαθός** | Regular 2‑1‑2 adjective endings. | τῶν διδομένων |
| **4. Deponents translate actively** | δυνάμενος "able," not "being enabled." | οἱ δυνάμενοι "those who are able" |
| **5. κείμενος serves as perfect passive of τίθημι** | Common prose idiom. | οἱ κείμενοι νόμοι "the established laws" |
| **6. Substantival use with the article** | The participle alone can act as a noun. | τὰ διδόμενα "the things given" |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **οἱ δυνάμενοι λέγειν πείθουσι τὸν δῆμον.** | Those able to speak persuade the people. (deponent) |
| **τὰ διδόμενα δῶρα δέχεται.** | He receives the gifts that are being given. (passive) |
| **οἱ καθήμενοι ἐν τῇ ἀγορᾷ ἤκουον.** | Those sitting in the marketplace were listening. |
| **κατὰ τοὺς κειμένους νόμους** | according to the established laws |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **δυνάμενος and κείμενος are extremely frequent** in historical and legal prose; recognizing ‑μενος instantly signals a middle/passive (or deponent) participle.
2. **With the article** the participle forms noun phrases central to Greek style: οἱ δυνάμενοι, τὰ γιγνόμενα.
3. **Circumstantial uses** (time, cause, concession) work exactly as with active participles.
