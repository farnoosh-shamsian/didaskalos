# The Aorist Optative Middle/Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γίγνομαι, βούλομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

A wish, a possibility, or a reported intention in past narrative, with the action seen as a single complete act. In the aorist the two voices are built differently:

| Voice | Formation | 3 sg | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Middle | ‑σαι‑ + secondary middle endings | **λύσαιτο** | may he ransom |
| Passive | ‑θει‑ + secondary active endings | **λυθείη** | may he be freed |

A "middle/passive" tag usually points at a deponent — γένοιτο, βουληθείη — whose form is middle or passive but whose sense is active.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Aorist Optative Middle: **λυσαίμην**

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λυσαίμην** | may I ransom |
| 2 sg | **λύσαιο** | may you ransom |
| 3 sg | **λύσαιτο** | may he ransom |
| 1 pl | **λυσαίμεθα** | may we ransom |
| 2 pl | **λύσαισθε** | may you ransom |
| 3 pl | **λύσαιντο** | may they ransom |

Thematic aorists drop the σ: γενοίμην, γένοιο, **γένοιτο**, γενοίμεθα, γένοισθε, γένοιντο.

### 2.2 Aorist Optative Passive: **λυθείην**

λυθείην, λυθείης, **λυθείη**, λυθεῖμεν, λυθεῖτε, λυθεῖεν — the ‑θει‑ stem with secondary active endings.

### 2.3 γένοιτο

**γένοιτο** "may it happen / it might be" is the single commonest form in this lesson, and μὴ γένοιτο "may it not happen — God forbid" is a set phrase in oratory and in the New Testament.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. αι and ει are the mood markers** | λύσ‑**αι**‑το (middle), λυ‑**θει**‑η (passive). |
| **2. No augment** | ἐλυσάμην, ἐλύθην are indicative. |
| **3. Potential with ἄν** | γένοιτο ἄν "it might happen". Without ἄν the same form is a wish. |
| **4. Secondary sequence** | After a past main verb the subjunctive becomes optative: ἐφοβεῖτο μὴ λυθείη. |
| **5. Optional forms in the plural** | λυθεῖμεν and λυθείημεν are both correct; Attic prefers the shorter set. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **μὴ γένοιτο.** — "May it not happen."
- **εἴθε λυσαίμεθα τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους.** — "If only we might ransom the prisoners!"
- **οὐκ ἂν βουληθείη ταῦτα ποιῆσαι.** — "He would not want to do this."
- **ἐφοβοῦντο μὴ ἡ πόλις ἁλοίη.** — "They feared the city might be captured."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Wishes and prayers** are introduced by εἴθε or εἰ γάρ, or stand alone as γένοιτο.
2. **The potential optative with ἄν** is the polite register of Attic prose: "one might say", "it would not be easy".
3. **Reading tip.** σαι + middle ending = middle; θει + ‑ην/‑η = passive. Neither takes an augment.
