# The Future Optative Active (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, ποιέω, γράφω)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

Like its middle counterpart, the future optative active exists for **one purpose only**: to represent a future indicative of direct speech inside an indirect construction after a past main verb.

- Direct: **λύσει** "he will free"
- Reported: **εἶπεν ὅτι λύσοι** "he said that he would free"

It is never a wish and never appears in a purpose clause. Greek could equally keep the original future indicative; the optative simply marks the report as belonging to past narrative.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigm: **λύσοιμι**

**future stem in ‑σ‑ + optative ‑οι‑ + secondary active endings**, with no augment:

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λύσοιμι** | (that) I would free |
| 2 sg | **λύσοις** | (that) you would free |
| 3 sg | **λύσοι** | (that) he would free |
| 1 pl | **λύσοιμεν** | (that) we would free |
| 2 pl | **λύσοιτε** | (that) you would free |
| 3 pl | **λύσοιεν** | (that) they would free |

### 2.2 Contract and Liquid Futures

Verbs whose future contracts show it here too:

| Verb | Future | Future optative 3 sg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ποιέω | ποιήσω | **ποιήσοι** |
| μένω | μενῶ | **μενοῖ** |
| ἀγγέλλω | ἀγγελῶ | **ἀγγελοῖ** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Indirect discourse only** | After ὅτι, ὡς, or in an indirect question, following a past main verb. |
| **2. ‑σοι‑ is the signature** | λύ‑**σοι**‑μι: σ for future, οι for optative. |
| **3. λύσοι vs. λύσαι** | λύσοι is the future optative; λύσαι is the aorist optative 3 sg. One vowel, two tenses. |
| **4. A future seen from the past** | It reports what was *going to* happen, from the standpoint of the past narrative — not from the speaker's present: "he said he would come". |
| **5. It is optional** | Retaining the future indicative (εἶπεν ὅτι λύσει) is equally good Greek. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **εἶπεν ὅτι ταῦτα ποιήσοι.** — "He said that he would do this."
- **ἠρώτων εἰ ἡ στρατιὰ μενοῖ.** — "They kept asking whether the army would remain."
- **ἤλπιζον ὅτι οἱ σύμμαχοι βοηθήσοιεν.** — "They hoped the allies would help."
- **οὐκ ἐδήλωσε τί γράψοι.** — "He did not make clear what he would write."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Xenophon and Thucydides** use it freely in reported speech; it is a marker of careful classical prose.
2. **Indirect questions** after a past verb are its second home: εἰ … μενοῖ.
3. **Reading tip.** ‑σοι‑ with active endings in a ὅτι clause after a past verb: future optative. It reports what was going to happen, viewed from the past narrative rather than from the present.
