# The Aorist Subjunctive Middle (ω-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Aorist Subjunctive Middle Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Aorist** | The action viewed as a single whole — aspect, not past time (outside the indicative the aorist has no augment and no past meaning). |
| **Subjunctive** | The mood of possibility, purpose, exhortation, and prohibition. |
| **Middle** | The subject acts on or for itself — or the verb is a deponent with active meaning. |
| **(w)** | The thematic verbs in ‑ω (λύω) and the deponents in ‑ομαι. |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation: Aorist Stem + Lengthened Thematic Vowel + Middle Endings

The subjunctive is marked by the **long thematic vowel** (ω/η). No augment.

**First (sigmatic) aorist: λύω → λύσωμαι:**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1st | **λύσωμαι** | **λυσώμεθα** |
| 2nd | **λύσῃ** | **λύσησθε** |
| 3rd | **λύσηται** | **λύσωνται** |

**Thematic aorist: γίγνομαι → ἐγενόμην → γένωμαι (deponent, "become"):**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1st | **γένωμαι** | **γενώμεθα** |
| 2nd | **γένῃ** | **γένησθε** |
| 3rd | **γένηται** | **γένωνται** |

*The thematic aorist subjunctive uses the bare aorist stem (γεν‑, λαβ‑) with the same endings: λάβωμαι, ἀφίκωμαι.*

### 2.2 Common Deponent Aorist Subjunctives

| Verb | Aorist | Subjunctive (3rd sg.) | Meaning |
|------|--------|-----------------------|---------|
| γίγνομαι | ἐγενόμην | **γένηται** | it may happen |
| ἀφικνέομαι | ἀφικόμην | **ἀφίκηται** | he may arrive |
| δέχομαι | ἐδεξάμην | **δέξηται** | he may receive |
| αἰσθάνομαι | ᾐσθόμην | **αἴσθηται** | he may perceive |

---

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. No augment** | The augment belongs to the indicative only. | γένηται, not *ἐγένηται |
| **2. Long vowel is the mood sign** | ω/η where the indicative has ο/ε. | λύσηται vs. (ἐ)λύσατο |
| **3. Aspect, not time** | Aorist subjunctive = single act; present subjunctive = process. | ἵνα γένηται "so that it may happen (once)" |
| **4. Purpose clauses** | ἵνα / ὅπως / ὡς + subjunctive. | ἵνα δέξηται τὰ δῶρα |
| **5. Fear and warning clauses** | μή + subjunctive after verbs of fearing. | φοβοῦμαι μὴ γένηται "I fear it may happen" |
| **6. Deliberation and exhortation** | 1st person: "what am I to…?", "let us…". | γενώμεθα ἀγαθοί "let us become good" |
| **7. 2nd sg. λύσῃ is ambiguous** | It matches the future middle indicative 2nd sg. — context decides. | — |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **ἔρχεται ἵνα δέξηται τὰ δῶρα.** | He comes so that he may receive the gifts. (deponent) |
| **φοβοῦνται μὴ γένηται πόλεμος.** | They fear that war may break out. (deponent) |
| **γενώμεθα ἄνδρες ἀγαθοί.** | Let us become good men. (hortatory) |
| **μένει ἕως ἂν ἀφίκηται ὁ ἄγγελος.** | He waits until the messenger arrives. |
| **ἐὰν λύσηται τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους, ἐπαινεθήσεται.** | If he ransoms the prisoners, he will be praised. (true middle: "have released for oneself") |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **γένηται is one of the commonest subjunctive forms in Greek prose** — conditional (ἐὰν γένηται), temporal (ὅταν γένηται), and purpose clauses live on it.
2. **λύσηται τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους** shows the classic middle force: "release *for one's own benefit*," i.e. ransom.
3. **Deponents dominate this category in real texts:** most aorist middle subjunctives you meet come from γίγνομαι, ἀφικνέομαι, δέχομαι and their kin — middle in form, active in meaning.
