# The Perfect Active Infinitive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, ποιέω)*

---

## 1. What the Perfect Active Infinitive Is

An infinitive names the action without tying it to a person. The **perfect** infinitive names it as *already completed, with the result standing*: λελυκέναι is not "to loose" but "**to have loosed**."

| Infinitive | Form | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | λύειν | to be loosing (ongoing) |
| Aorist | λῦσαι | to loose (simple event) |
| **Perfect** | **λελυκέναι** | to have loosed (completed, result stands) |

In Greek the choice among these is **aspect**, not time. Only in indirect statement does the perfect infinitive also carry a time reference — see §5.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**reduplicated perfect active stem + ‑έναι**

| Verb | Perfect indicative | Perfect infinitive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| λύω "loose" | λέλυκα | **λελυκέναι** |
| ποιέω "do, make" | πεποίηκα | **πεποιηκέναι** |
| γράφω "write" | γέγραφα | **γεγραφέναι** |
| πάσχω "suffer" | πέπονθα | **πεπονθέναι** |
| λείπω "leave" | λέλοιπα | **λελοιπέναι** |
| ἀκούω "hear" | ἀκήκοα | **ἀκηκοέναι** |

Verbs with a κ-perfect keep the κ (λελυ**κ**έναι); those without κ have none (γεγραφέναι).

### 2.2 Accent

The accent stands on the syllable **before ‑ναι** — that is, on the ‑έ‑ — in every perfect active infinitive, however long the word:

- λελυκ**έ**ναι, πεποιηκ**έ**ναι, ἀκηκο**έ**ναι

This is fixed. It does not follow the recessive pattern of finite verbs, and it is one of the surest ways to identify the form.

### 2.3 With the Article

Like any infinitive it can be turned into a noun with the neuter article:

- **τὸ λελυκέναι** — "the having loosed", the fact of having loosed
- **διὰ τὸ πεπονθέναι** — "because of having suffered"

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Reduplication is the marker** | Present λύειν, perfect λελυκέναι. If a reduplicated syllable stands in front, the form is perfect — no augment ever appears on an infinitive. |
| **2. No augment, ever** | The augment belongs to the indicative. ἐλελύκη is pluperfect indicative; λελυκέναι is the infinitive. Never *ἐλελυκέναι. |
| **3. ‑έναι, not ‑ειν** | λύειν (present) vs. λελυκέναι (perfect). The ending is longer and its accent is fixed. |
| **4. εἰδέναι is the one to know** | From οἶδα: εἰδέναι "to know" is perfect in form and present in meaning, and it is far commoner than any regular perfect infinitive. |
| **5. Distinguish from the middle/passive** | Active λελυκέναι "to have loosed"; middle/passive λελύσθαι "to have been loosed". Different ending, different voice. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **φησὶ τὸν ἄνδρα τεθνηκέναι.** — "He says the man has died."
- **ὁμολογοῦσι ταῦτα πεποιηκέναι.** — "They admit they have done this."
- **διὰ τὸ πολλὰ πεπονθέναι σοφώτερος ἐγένετο.** — "Through having suffered much he became wiser."
- **δοκεῖ μοι ἡ πόλις καλῶς βεβουλεῦσθαι.** — "It seems to me the city has deliberated well."
- **οὐδεὶς ὁμολογεῖ ἀδικεῖν, ἀλλὰ πάντες ἠδικηκέναι τοὺς ἄλλους φασίν.** — "No one admits to doing wrong; everyone says the others have done it."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Indirect statement** is where it lives. After verbs of saying and thinking (φημί, λέγω, νομίζω, ὁμολογέω), the infinitive keeps the tense of the original direct speech: "he has died" (τέθνηκε → τεθνηκέναι) reported becomes the perfect infinitive.
2. **Result and cause clauses.** With ὥστε and with the articular infinitive after διά, the perfect stresses that a state has been reached, not merely that something happened.
3. **Reading tip.** Two syllables tell you almost everything: a reduplication at the front and ‑έναι at the back. Between them lies the verb stem.
