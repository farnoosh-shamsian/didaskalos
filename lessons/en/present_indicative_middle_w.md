# The Present Indicative Middle (ω-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Present Indicative Middle Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Present** | An action or state occurring now or habitually. |
| **Indicative** | The "statement" mood — for facts and ordinary questions. |
| **Middle** | The subject is personally involved in the action: acting on itself, for its own benefit, or with its own participation. |
| **(w)** | The thematic verbs in ‑ω (λύω), including contract verbs and the deponents in ‑ομαι. |

In the present tense middle and passive **share the same forms**; this lesson gathers the tokens your corpus annotates specifically as **middle** — where the sense is "for oneself / with self‑involvement," or the verb is a deponent with active meaning.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Primary Middle Endings

| Person | Ending | λύω → | Meaning (middle) |
|--------|--------|-------|------------------|
| 1st sg. | **‑ομαι** | **λύομαι** | I loose for myself, ransom |
| 2nd sg. | **‑ῃ / ‑ει** | **λύῃ / λύει** | you… |
| 3rd sg. | **‑εται** | **λύεται** | he/she/it… |
| 1st pl. | **‑όμεθα** | **λυόμεθα** | we… |
| 2nd pl. | **‑εσθε** | **λύεσθε** | you (pl.)… |
| 3rd pl. | **‑ονται** | **λύονται** | they… |

*The 2nd singular arose from ‑ε‑σαι: the σ dropped between vowels and the vowels contracted (λύε‑σαι → λύῃ).*

### 2.2 The Three Faces of a Middle Form

| Sense | Example | Translation |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Direct reflexive** | λούομαι | I wash myself |
| **Indirect ("for oneself")** | λύομαι | I ransom (release for my own benefit) |
| **Deponent (active meaning)** | ἔρχομαι | I come |

### 2.3 Common Deponents in the Present

| Verb | Meaning | 3rd sg. |
|------|---------|---------|
| **ἔρχομαι** | to come, go | ἔρχεται |
| **γίγνομαι / γίνομαι** | to become, happen | γίγνεται / γίνεται |
| **βούλομαι** | to want | βούλεται |
| **δέχομαι** | to receive | δέχεται |
| **πορεύομαι** | to travel | πορεύεται |
| **ἀποκρίνομαι** | to answer | ἀποκρίνεται |

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. Same forms as the passive** | In the present, only context (or a deponent headword) decides. | λύεται "he ransoms" or "he is loosed" |
| **2. Deponents translate actively** | The headword in ‑ομαι is the signal. | ἔρχεται "he comes" |
| **3. Middles with special meanings** | Some active verbs shift sense in the middle. | ἄρχω "rule" / ἄρχομαι "begin"; πείθω "persuade" / πείθομαι "obey" |
| **4. κοινή spelling γίνομαι** | Later Greek drops the second γ. | γίνεται (NT) = γίγνεται (Attic) |
| **5. Contract deponents** | Stem vowel merges with the ending. | φοβέομαι → φοβεῖται |
| **6. No agent phrase with middles** | ὑπό + genitive signals a passive instead. | — |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **ἔρχεται ὁ διδάσκαλος εἰς τὴν πόλιν.** | The teacher is coming into the city. (deponent) |
| **βούλομαι μανθάνειν.** | I want to learn. (deponent) |
| **ταῦτα γίγνεται καθ' ἡμέραν.** | These things happen every day. (deponent) |
| **οἱ παῖδες λούονται ἐν τῷ ποταμῷ.** | The children wash themselves in the river. (reflexive middle) |
| **ὁ στρατηγὸς πείθεται τοῖς νόμοις.** | The general obeys the laws. (middle with special meaning) |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **In running prose most present middles are deponents** — ἔρχεται, γίγνεται, βούλεται carry whole narratives. Translate them actively without hesitation.
2. **The self‑interest nuance** of true middles rewards attention: λύεται "he ransoms" (for himself) vs. active λύει "he releases."
3. **Corpora that distinguish "middle" from "middle/passive"** (as the annotation of this sentence bank sometimes does) are telling you the annotator judged the sense middle, not passive — a useful reading hint.
