# The Aorist Imperative Active (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What the Aorist Active Imperative Is

A command to perform **one complete act**: δός "give it", θές "put it down", στῆθι "halt".

The contrast with the present imperative is aspect, not time — neither form commands anything in the past:

| | Form | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | δίδου | "keep giving", be giving |
| **Aorist** | **δός** | "give it" — one act, done |

For μι-verbs these are **root aorist** forms: the ending goes straight onto the bare stem, so the results are among the shortest words in Greek.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Four Main Verbs

| Person | δίδωμι "give" | τίθημι "place" | ἵημι "send" | ἵστημι "stand" |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **δός** | **θές** | **ἕς** | **στῆθι** |
| 3 sg | **δότω** | **θέτω** | **ἕτω** | **στήτω** |
| 2 pl | **δότε** | **θέτε** | **ἕτε** | **στῆτε** |
| 3 pl | **δόντων** | **θέντων** | **ἕντων** | **στάντων** |

δίδωμι, τίθημι and ἵημι take the bare-stem 2nd singular (δός, θές, ἕς); ἵστημι and other intransitive root aorists take **‑θι**: στῆθι, and likewise γνῶθι (γιγνώσκω) "know", βῆθι (βαίνω) "go".

> **γνῶθι σαυτόν** — "Know thyself", the inscription at Delphi, is an aorist imperative of exactly this type.

### 2.2 Compounds and the Accent

Simple δός, θές, ἕς are accented on the ending. In compounds the accent **recedes onto the preposition**, and this shift is a reliable clue:

| Compound | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ἀποδίδωμι | **ἀπόδος** | give back! |
| παραδίδωμι | **παράδος** | hand over! |
| ἐπιτίθημι | **ἐπίθες** | set upon! |
| προστίθημι | **πρόσθες** | add! |
| ἀφίημι | **ἄφες** | let go! release! |

### 2.3 Prohibitions

There is no negative aorist imperative. To forbid a single act, Greek uses **μή + aorist subjunctive**:

- **μὴ δῷς** — "don't give"
- **μὴ θῇς** — "don't put"
- but **μὴ δίδου** — "don't keep giving" (present imperative)

---

## 3. Key Rules and Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἔδωκα, ἔθηκα are indicative; the imperative is δός, θές. An imperative never carries the augment. |
| **2. Short vowel, bare stem** | δο‑, θε‑, ἑ‑, στα‑. The κ of ἔδωκα and ἔθηκα belongs to the indicative singular only and never appears here. |
| **3. δός vs. δίδου** | One letter of difference in meaning-weight: δός is a single act, δίδου an ongoing practice. Editors and translators take the distinction seriously. |
| **4. ἄφες is everywhere in Koine** | "Forgive, let go" — ἄφες ἡμῖν τὰ ὀφειλήματα ἡμῶν in the Lord's Prayer is this form. |
| **5. στῆθι is intransitive** | ἵστημι's root aorist means "stand, halt", not "make something stand". The transitive command is στῆσον (sigmatic aorist). |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **δός μοι τὸ βιβλίον.** — "Give me the book."
- **ἀπόδος τὰ χρήματα τοῖς πολίταις.** — "Give the money back to the citizens."
- **θὲς τὰ ὅπλα χαμαί.** — "Put the weapons on the ground."
- **γνῶθι σαυτόν.** — "Know thyself."
- **ἄφες αὐτοὺς ἀπελθεῖν.** — "Let them go away."
- **στῆτε καὶ ἀκούσατε.** — "Halt and listen."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Short, sharp, and everywhere.** These are the commands of dialogue, prayer and command on the battlefield; their brevity is exactly why they are frequent.
2. **The compound is usually the word.** ἀπόδος, παράδος, ἐπίθες, ἄφες occur far more often than the simple forms — and the retreating accent is what identifies them.
3. **Reading tip.** A one-syllable word ending in ‑ς that will not parse as a noun is very likely one of δός, θές, ἕς, or their compounds.
