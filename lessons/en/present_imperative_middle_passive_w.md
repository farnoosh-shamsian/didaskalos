# The Present Imperative Middle/Passive (ω-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Present Imperative Middle/Passive Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Present** | Aspect of process or repetition: "keep doing," "do habitually." |
| **Imperative** | The mood of command and request (2nd person) or of "let him/them…" (3rd person). |
| **Middle** | The subject acts on or for itself: "wash yourself!" |
| **Passive** | The subject is to receive the action: "be persuaded!" |
| **(w)** | The thematic verbs in ‑ω (λύω), including contract verbs and the deponents in ‑ομαι. |

The present imperative commands an **ongoing or general** action; the aorist imperative commands a **single** act.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Endings

| Person | Ending | λύω → | Meaning |
|--------|--------|-------|---------|
| 2nd sg. | **‑ου** (from ‑ε‑σο) | **λύου** | be loosed! |
| 3rd sg. | **‑έσθω** | **λυέσθω** | let him… |
| 2nd pl. | **‑εσθε** | **λύεσθε** | (you all)…! |
| 3rd pl. | **‑έσθων / ‑έσθωσαν** | **λυέσθων** | let them… |

*The 2nd singular ‑ου arose when the σ of ‑ε‑σο dropped between vowels and ε + ο contracted: λύε‑σο → λύε‑ο → λύου.*

*Note: the 2nd plural λύεσθε is identical to the indicative; only context shows a command.*

### 2.2 Contract Verbs

| Verb | 2nd sg. | 2nd pl. |
|------|---------|---------|
| φοβέομαι "fear" | **φοβοῦ** | **φοβεῖσθε** |
| αἰτιάομαι "accuse" | **αἰτιῶ** | **αἰτιᾶσθε** |

### 2.3 Deponent Imperatives

Deponents use exactly these endings, translated **actively**:

| Verb | 2nd sg. | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| **γίγνομαι** | **γίγνου** | become! |
| **ἔρχομαι** | **ἔρχου** | come! |
| **δέχομαι** | **δέχου** | receive! |
| **βούλομαι** | **βούλου** | wish! |
| **πορεύομαι** | **πορεύου** | travel! go! |

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. Present = keep doing / general rule** | Contrast the aorist imperative for a single act. | μὴ φοβοῦ "don't be (habitually) afraid" |
| **2. Negative is μή** | Never οὐ with imperatives. | μὴ ἔρχου "don't come" |
| **3. μή + present imperative can mean "stop…!"** | Ordering the cessation of an ongoing act. | μὴ μάχεσθε "stop fighting!" |
| **4. 2nd pl. looks like the indicative** | λύεσθε is command or statement by context. | — |
| **5. Deponents translate actively** | Middle form, active command. | πορεύου "march!" |
| **6. No 1st person** | Exhortation "let us…" uses the subjunctive instead. | λυώμεθα |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **μὴ φοβοῦ, ὦ παῖ.** | Don't be afraid, child. (deponent) |
| **ἔρχου εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν.** | Come into the house. (deponent) |
| **δέχεσθε τὰ δῶρα.** | Receive the gifts. (deponent) |
| **πειθέσθω τοῖς νόμοις ὁ πολίτης.** | Let the citizen obey the laws. (3rd person) |
| **λούου καθ' ἡμέραν.** | Wash yourself every day. (true middle) |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **Commands to "be" and "become"** (γίγνου) and prohibitions with μὴ φοβοῦ are among the commonest imperatives in Greek literature.
2. **Present vs. aorist imperative** is aspect, not time: μὴ φοβοῦ "don't go on fearing" vs. μὴ φοβηθῇς "don't take fright (now)."
3. **3rd‑person imperatives** are a normal feature of laws and prayers: "let him be fined," "let it be done."
