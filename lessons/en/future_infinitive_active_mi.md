# The Future Active Infinitive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, δείκνυμι)*

---

## 1. What This Infinitive Is

"To be going to give", "to be about to place". The future infinitive is unusual among infinitives in that it **does** carry time: it appears almost exclusively where the time of the action matters — in indirect statement and after verbs of hoping, promising and swearing.

In the future, μι-verbs behave exactly like ω-verbs. The athematic stem lengthens, σ is added, and everything after that is regular:

| Verb | Future indicative | Future infinitive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι "give" | δώσω | **δώσειν** |
| τίθημι "place" | θήσω | **θήσειν** |
| ἵστημι "set up" | στήσω | **στήσειν** |
| ἵημι "send" | ἥσω | **ἥσειν** |
| δείκνυμι "show" | δείξω | **δείξειν** |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**long stem vowel + ‑σ‑ + ‑ειν.** The long vowel that appears in the first person singular of the present (δίδω‑μι, τίθη‑μι) is the one the future uses throughout.

### 2.2 Compounds

| Compound | Infinitive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ἀποδίδωμι | **ἀποδώσειν** | to be going to give back |
| παραδίδωμι | **παραδώσειν** | to be going to hand over |
| ἐπιτίθημι | **ἐπιθήσειν** | to be going to set upon |
| ἀφίημι | **ἀφήσειν** | to be going to release |

### 2.3 Where It Is Used

| Construction | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Indirect statement | ἔφη δώσειν "he said he would give" |
| After verbs of hoping | ἐλπίζω δώσειν "I hope to give" |
| After promising, swearing | ὑπέσχετο ἀποδώσειν "he promised to give back" |
| With μέλλω | μέλλει δώσειν "he is about to give" |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. It really does mean future time** | Unlike the present and aorist infinitives, which mark aspect, δώσειν after a verb of saying reports "he will give". |
| **2. Long vowel plus σ** | δώσειν, θήσειν, στήσειν. The short stems (δο‑, θε‑, στα‑) belong to the aorist: δοῦναι, θεῖναι, στῆναι. |
| **3. Distinguish from the aorist infinitive** | δώσειν "to be going to give" vs. δοῦναι "to give". Confusing them changes when the action happens. |
| **4. μέλλω usually takes the future** | μέλλω δώσειν is the standard idiom for imminent action; μέλλω δοῦναι occurs but is less common. |
| **5. No augment anywhere** | The future has no past reference of its own and never takes an augment. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ὑπέσχετο τὰ χρήματα ἀποδώσειν.** — "He promised to give the money back."
- **ἔφασαν τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους παραδώσειν.** — "They said they would hand over the prisoners."
- **ἐλπίζομεν τρόπαιον στήσειν.** — "We hope to set up a trophy."
- **ἔμελλε τὸν νόμον θήσειν.** — "He was about to lay down the law."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Treaties and negotiations** run on this form: what each side undertakes to do is reported with a future infinitive after ὑπισχνέομαι or ὄμνυμι.
2. **In indirect statement** the future infinitive preserves the tense of the original: "I will give" (δώσω) reported becomes δώσειν whatever the tense of the main verb.
3. **Reading tip.** Long vowel + σ + ειν, and no augment: the future active infinitive, whether the verb is a μι-verb or not.
