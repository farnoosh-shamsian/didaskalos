# The Aorist Optative Passive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, ἀφίημι)*

---

## 1. What This Optative Is

A wish or possibility, or — most often — a purpose or fear clause reported in past narrative, with the subject undergoing a single completed action: ἵνα δοθείη "so that it might be given".

Built on the familiar ‑θε‑ passive stem, so if you know ἐδόθην you can form it at once.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigm: **δοθείην** "may I be given"

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **δοθείην** | may I be given |
| 2 sg | **δοθείης** | may you be given |
| 3 sg | **δοθείη** | may it be given |
| 1 pl | **δοθεῖμεν** (δοθείημεν) | may we be given |
| 2 pl | **δοθεῖτε** (δοθείητε) | may you be given |
| 3 pl | **δοθεῖεν** (δοθείησαν) | may they be given |

The other stems behave identically: **τεθείη**, **σταθείη**, **ἀφεθείη**, **δειχθείη**.

### 2.2 Uses

| Construction | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose in past narrative | ἔπεμψαν ἵνα ἡ χώρα δοθείη "they sent so that the land might be given" |
| Fear clause | ἐφοβοῦντο μὴ ἀφεθείη "they feared he might be released" |
| Wish | εἴθε σταθείη τὸ τρόπαιον "may the trophy be set up" |
| Potential (with ἄν) | δοθείη ἄν "it might be given" |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. ‑θει‑ + ‑η‑ is the signature** | δο‑**θει**‑**η**‑ν: θ for the passive, ει/ιη for the optative. |
| **2. No augment** | ἐδόθην is indicative; δοθείην is optative. |
| **3. Short and long plurals** | δοθεῖεν and δοθείησαν are both correct; Attic prefers the shorter. |
| **4. Distinguish from the participle** | δοθείς, δοθέντος (participle, takes case endings) vs. δοθείη, δοθεῖεν (optative, takes person endings). |
| **5. Secondary sequence** | The subjunctive δοθῇ of direct speech becomes δοθείη after a past main verb. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἐφοβοῦντο μὴ ἡ πόλις τοῖς πολεμίοις δοθείη.** — "They feared the city might be handed over to the enemy."
- **εἴθε οἱ νόμοι καλῶς τεθεῖεν.** — "If only the laws might be well established!"
- **ἔπεμψαν πρέσβεις ἵνα οἱ αἰχμάλωτοι ἀφεθεῖεν.** — "They sent envoys so that the prisoners might be released."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Past-tense purpose and fear clauses** in the historians are where nearly all these forms occur.
2. **The potential with ἄν** softens an assertion: "it might be given" rather than "it will be".
3. **Reading tip.** θει plus a personal ending and no augment: aorist passive optative.
