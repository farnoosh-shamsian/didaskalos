# The Future Optative Middle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

The future optative exists for one reason: to represent a **future indicative of direct speech** inside an indirect construction after a past main verb.

- Direct: **δώσεται** "he will give (of his own)"
- Reported: **εἶπεν ὅτι δώσοιτο** "he said that he would give"

It is never a wish and never a purpose clause. For μι-verbs the future is entirely regular, so these forms hold no surprises beyond their rarity.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Paradigm: **δωσοίμην**

**long stem vowel + ‑σ‑ + optative ‑οι‑ + secondary middle endings**:

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **δωσοίμην** | (that) I would give |
| 2 sg | **δώσοιο** | (that) you would give |
| 3 sg | **δώσοιτο** | (that) he would give |
| 1 pl | **δωσοίμεθα** | (that) we would give |
| 2 pl | **δώσοισθε** | (that) you would give |
| 3 pl | **δώσοιντο** | (that) they would give |

τίθημι gives θησοίμην, θήσοιο, θήσοιτο; ἵστημι gives στησοίμην, στήσοιο, στήσοιτο.

### 2.2 Compounds

ἀποδωσοίμην "that I would sell", ἐπιθησοίμην "that I would attack", συνθησοίμην "that I would agree".

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Indirect discourse only** | After ὅτι, ὡς, or in indirect questions, following a past main verb. |
| **2. σοι is the signature** | δω‑**σοι**‑το: σ for the future, οι for the optative. |
| **3. Long stem vowel** | δω‑, θη‑, στη‑: the aorist optatives are the short δοίμην, θείμην. |
| **4. δώσοιτο vs. δοῖτο** | Future "would give" vs. aorist "might give". The σ and the long vowel mark the future. |
| **5. Retaining the indicative is equally good** | εἶπεν ὅτι δώσεται is just as correct as εἶπεν ὅτι δώσοιτο. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **εἶπεν ὅτι τὴν χώραν οὐκ ἀποδώσοιτο.** — "He said that he would not sell the land."
- **ἠρώτων εἰ σπονδὰς θήσοιντο.** — "They asked whether they would make a truce."
- **ἤλπιζον ὅτι οἱ σύμμαχοι συνθήσοιντο.** — "They hoped the allies would come to terms."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Classical Attic prose**, especially Xenophon, is where this form lives; Koine has effectively lost it.
2. **Indirect questions** with εἰ are its second home.
3. **Reading tip.** Long vowel + σοι + middle ending inside a ὅτι clause after a past verb: future optative. It reports what was going to happen, viewed from the past narrative rather than from the present.
