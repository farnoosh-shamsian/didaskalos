# The Perfect Optative Active (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, οἶδα)*

---

## 1. What This Optative Is

A wish or possibility concerning a **completed state**: "may he have freed", "if he should know". It is the rarest optative in the language, and Greek usually avoids the simple form altogether.

| Type | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Periphrastic (usual) | **λελυκὼς εἴην** | may I have freed |
| Simple (rare) | **λελύκοιμι** | may I have freed |
| Common lexical case | **εἰδείην** | may I know |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Periphrasis

**perfect active participle + optative of εἰμί** (εἴην, εἴης, εἴη, εἶμεν, εἶτε, εἶεν):

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λελυκὼς εἴην** |
| 3 sg | **λελυκὼς εἴη** |
| 3 pl | **λελυκότες εἶεν** |

This is the normal way to express the idea, and it is what you should expect to meet.

### 2.2 The Simple Forms

Where they exist, they are built with ‑οι‑ on the perfect stem: λελύκοιμι, λελύκοις, λελύκοι. They are largely confined to a few verbs and to poetry.

### 2.3 εἰδείην

By far the commonest member of this class is the optative of **οἶδα**:

| Person | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **εἰδείην** | may I know / that I might know |
| 3 sg | **εἰδείη** | that he might know |
| 3 pl | **εἰδεῖεν** | that they might know |

Since οἶδα means "I know", εἰδείη is a present in force and belongs to ordinary prose, not to rare poetic usage.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Expect the periphrasis** | λελυκὼς εἴη, not λελύκοι, is the everyday Greek. |
| **2. εἰδείη is a present** | Translate "might know", never "might have known". |
| **3. Reported knowledge** | οὐκ ᾔδει ὅ τι εἰδείη is typical: an οἶδα of direct speech becomes εἰδείη after a past verb. |
| **4. No augment** | Optatives never take one; the augment belongs to the pluperfect indicative. |
| **5. Do not confuse with the aorist optative** | λύσαι (aorist) vs. λελύκοι (perfect): reduplication marks the perfect. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **οὐκ ἔφη εἰδέναι οὐδὲ εἰκάσαι ἂν ὅ τι εἰδείη.** — "He said he did not know, nor could he guess what he might know."
- **εἴθε ταῦτα γεγραφὼς εἴην.** — "If only I had written this."
- **ἠρώτησαν εἰ οἱ στρατιῶται τὰ ὅπλα παρειληφότες εἶεν.** — "They asked whether the soldiers had received the arms."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Indirect discourse after past verbs** accounts for nearly every occurrence of this form.
2. **εἰδείην and its plural** are the members worth memorizing; the rest can be recognized from the periphrasis.
3. **Reading tip.** A perfect participle sitting beside εἴη or εἶεν is a single perfect optative; read them together.
