# The Pluperfect Indicative Middle/Passive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

The perfect middle/passive says "it **has been** given, and it is theirs now". The pluperfect moves that to the past: "it **had been** given, and it was theirs then".

| Tense | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perfect | δέδοται | it has been given |
| **Pluperfect** | **ἐδέδοτο** | it had been given |

The third singular is by far the commonest form: a state of affairs already in place when the narrative reaches it.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**augment + reduplicated stem + secondary middle endings**, attached directly with no thematic vowel — the hallmark of the μι-verbs.

### 2.2 Paradigm: **δίδωμι** → **ἐδεδόμην** "I had been given"

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **ἐδεδόμην** | I had been given |
| 2 sg | **ἐδέδοσο** | you had been given |
| 3 sg | **ἐδέδοτο** | he/she/it had been given |
| 1 pl | **ἐδεδόμεθα** | we had been given |
| 2 pl | **ἐδέδοσθε** | you (pl.) had been given |
| 3 pl | **ἐδέδοντο** | they had been given |

τίθημι gives ἐτεθείμην, ἐτέθεισο, ἐτέθειτο…; ἵστημι gives **εἱστάμην**, εἵστασο, εἵστατο "I was standing", with the augment showing as εἱ‑.

### 2.3 Consonant Stems Take a Periphrasis

When the stem ends in a consonant, the third plural cannot add ‑ντο (the cluster is unpronounceable), so Greek uses the perfect participle with the imperfect of εἰμί:

- **δεδειγμένοι ἦσαν** — "they had been shown"

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Augment *and* reduplication** | ἐ‑δέ‑δοτο. Perfect δέδοται has the reduplication only. |
| **2. Endings attach directly** | ἐδέδο‑το, not *ἐδεδό‑ε‑το. ω-verbs show the same: ἐλέλυ‑το. |
| **3. εἱστάμην means "I was standing"** | ἵστημι's perfect system is intransitive, so its pluperfect is an ordinary past: "was standing", not "had been set up". |
| **4. 2 sg keeps its σ** | ἐδέδοσο, not *ἐδέδου: the athematic forms keep ‑σο intact, where the thematic imperfect lost the σ and contracted (ἐλύου). |
| **5. Rare, but easy once seen** | The pluperfect middle/passive is uncommon; when it appears it is nearly always 3 sg or 3 pl reporting a settled state. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἡ χώρα τοῖς συμμάχοις ἐδέδοτο.** — "The land had been given to the allies."
- **οἱ νόμοι ἤδη ἐτέθειντο.** — "The laws had already been laid down."
- **εἱστήκεσαν οἱ φύλακες ἔνθα ἐτέτακτο.** — "The guards were standing where it had been ordered."
- **τὰ ὅπλα τοῖς στρατιώταις δεδομένα ἦν.** — "The weapons had been given to the soldiers." (periphrastic)

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Scene-setting in historians.** A pluperfect middle/passive tells you what had already been arranged before the events of the sentence began.
2. **Periphrastic forms are common** even where a simple form exists; do not be surprised by participle + ἦν.
3. **Reading tip.** Augment, reduplication, and a middle ending attached with no linking vowel: that combination is this tense and nothing else.
