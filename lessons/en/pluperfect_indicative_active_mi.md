# The Pluperfect Indicative Active (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What the Pluperfect Indicative Active Is

The pluperfect takes the perfect's completed action, with its standing result, and places the whole picture **in the past**: "I had given", "he was standing".

| Tense | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aorist | ἔδωκα | I gave |
| Perfect | δέδωκα | I have given (it is theirs now) |
| **Pluperfect** | **ἐδεδώκη** | I had given (it was theirs then) |

For μι-verbs the most useful member of this tense is **εἱστήκη** "I was standing" — the past of ἕστηκα "I stand", and far commoner in narrative than any other pluperfect.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**augment + reduplicated perfect stem + ‑κ‑ + pluperfect endings** (‑η, ‑ης, ‑ει, ‑εμεν, ‑ετε, ‑εσαν; later ‑ειν, ‑εις in the singular).

### 2.2 Paradigm: **δίδωμι** → **ἐδεδώκη** "I had given"

| Person | Form | Later variant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **ἐδεδώκη** | ἐδεδώκειν |
| 2 sg | **ἐδεδώκης** | ἐδεδώκεις |
| 3 sg | **ἐδεδώκει(ν)** | — |
| 1 pl | **ἐδεδώκεμεν** | — |
| 2 pl | **ἐδεδώκετε** | — |
| 3 pl | **ἐδεδώκεσαν** | — |

τίθημι gives ἐτεθείκη, ἐτεθείκης, ἐτεθείκει…

### 2.3 ἵστημι: **εἱστήκη** "I was standing"

Because ἕστηκα means "I stand", its pluperfect means "I stood, I was standing" — an imperfect in force:

| Person | Long | Short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **εἱστήκη** | — |
| 3 sg | **εἱστήκει** | — |
| 1 pl | εἱστήκεμεν | **ἕσταμεν** (perfect used for present) |
| 3 pl | εἱστήκεσαν | **ἕστασαν** |

The augment of ἕστηκα appears as the diphthong **εἱ‑**: ἑ‑ + ἐ‑ contract into εἱ‑.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Augment *and* reduplication** | ἐ‑δε‑δώκη carries both. That double marking is the surest sign of a pluperfect. |
| **2. εἱστήκη = "was standing"** | Translate it as an imperfect, never as "had stood". The same holds for ἐτεθνήκη "he was dead". |
| **3. The endings are unique** | ‑η, ‑ης, ‑ει, ‑εμεν, ‑ετε, ‑εσαν belong to this tense alone — they are neither imperfect nor aorist endings. |
| **4. Rare outside these verbs** | Beyond εἱστήκη, ᾔδη and a few others, the pluperfect is uncommon; Greek narrates with the aorist and lets sequence do the work. |
| **5. Indicative only** | There is no pluperfect subjunctive, optative or imperative in ordinary use. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἐδεδώκει τὰ χρήματα τοῖς στρατιώταις πρὶν ἀπελθεῖν.** — "He had given the money to the soldiers before departing."
- **εἱστήκεσαν οἱ ὁπλῖται ἐν τάξει.** — "The hoplites were standing in formation."
- **ἐτεθείκεσαν τοὺς νόμους οἱ πρόγονοι.** — "Our ancestors had laid down the laws."
- **οὐκ ᾔδη ταῦτα πρότερον.** — "I had not known this before."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Battle description.** εἱστήκει and εἱστήκεσαν are the standard way to say where troops were positioned when something else happened.
2. **Temporal clauses.** With ἐπεί and ἐπειδή the pluperfect marks one action as already finished when the next began.
3. **Reading tip.** Look for augment + reduplication + κ. If the verb is ἵστημι, expect εἱ‑ at the front and translate as a simple past.
