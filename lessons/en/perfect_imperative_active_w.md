# The Perfect Imperative Active (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, ἵστημι, οἶδα)*

---

## 1. What This Imperative Is

A command that a **state be in force**, not that an act be performed: "be in the condition of having done it".

It is the rarest imperative in Greek, and for most ω-verbs it is not formed with a simple ending at all. Where a verb needs one, Greek uses a **periphrasis**: the perfect active participle with the imperative of εἰμί.

| Type | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Periphrastic | **λελυκὼς ἴσθι** | be in the state of having freed |
| Simple (rare) | **ἕσταθι**, **τέθναθι** | stand!, be dead! |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Periphrastic Form

**perfect active participle + imperative of εἰμί**, agreeing with the addressee:

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **λελυκὼς ἴσθι** |
| 3 sg | **λελυκὼς ἔστω** |
| 2 pl | **λελυκότες ἔστε** |
| 3 pl | **λελυκότες ἔστων** |

### 2.2 The Simple Forms That Do Exist

They belong almost entirely to perfects with present meaning:

| Verb | Imperative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| οἶδα "know" | **ἴσθι**, pl. **ἴστε** | know! |
| ἵστημι "stand" | **ἕσταθι**, pl. **ἕστατε** | stand! |
| θνῄσκω "die" | **τέθναθι** | be dead! |
| κέκραγα "shout" | **κέκραχθι** | shout! |

Note that **ἴσθι** does double duty: "know!" from οἶδα and "be!" from εἰμί.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Expect a periphrasis** | For an ordinary ω-verb, participle + ἴσθι/ἔστω is the normal way to give this command. |
| **2. The simple forms are lexical** | ἴσθι, ἕσταθι, τέθναθι must be learned as vocabulary; they are not a productive pattern. |
| **3. State, not act** | Compare aorist λῦσον "free him" with λελυκὼς ἴσθι "have him freed / see that he is freed". |
| **4. Not to be confused with the middle** | The perfect middle/passive imperative (λέλυσο, μέμνησο, εἰρήσθω) is a separate and rather commoner set. |
| **5. ἴσθι is ambiguous** | Only the construction tells you whether it belongs to οἶδα or to εἰμί. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἴσθι τοῦτο σαφῶς.** — "Know this clearly."
- **ἴστε ὅτι οὐ ψεύδομαι.** — "Be assured that I am not lying."
- **ἕσταθι παρὰ τὴν θύραν.** — "Stand by the door."
- **τεθνάτω ὁ προδότης.** — "Let the traitor be dead." (3 sg)
- **λελυκὼς ἔστω ὁ δεσμώτης πρὶν ἑσπέρας.** — "Let the prisoner have been freed before evening."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **ἴσθι and ἴστε** are extremely common in oratory: "know well that…" is a standard appeal to a jury.
2. **Tragedy** supplies most of the τέθναθι type, where the state commanded is a final one.
3. **Reading tip.** If you meet a perfect participle next to ἴσθι, ἔστω or ἔστε, read the two together as one imperative.
