# The Aorist Middle/Passive Infinitive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, δύναμαι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

In the aorist the middle and the passive are separate formations, and μι-verbs keep them as far apart as ω-verbs do:

| Voice | Formation | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Middle | bare stem + ‑σθαι | **δόσθαι** | to give of one's own |
| Passive | stem + ‑θῆναι | **δοθῆναι** | to be given |

A row tagged "middle/passive" therefore covers two shapes. In practice it usually marks a **deponent** — a verb with no active forms of its own, whose meaning is active — or a form the annotation left open.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Side by Side

| Verb | Middle infinitive | Passive infinitive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι | **δόσθαι** | **δοθῆναι** |
| τίθημι | **θέσθαι** | **τεθῆναι** |
| ἵημι | **ἕσθαι** | **ἑθῆναι** (ἀφεθῆναι) |
| ἵστημι | **στήσασθαι** | **σταθῆναι** |

### 2.2 The Deponents

The μι-verbs include several deponents whose aorist infinitive is active in meaning:

| Verb | Aorist infinitive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δύναμαι "be able" | **δυνηθῆναι** | to be able |
| ἐπίσταμαι "know how" | **ἐπιστηθῆναι** | to understand |
| ὀνίναμαι "benefit" | **ὄνασθαι** | to enjoy, to profit |

δυνηθῆναι shows the pattern clearly: passive in form, active in sense.

### 2.3 Accent

Middle: on the first syllable of the simple form (δόσθαι, θέσθαι), no further back than the preposition in compounds (ἀποδόσθαι). Passive: fixed circumflex on ‑θῆ‑ (δοθῆναι, ἀποδοθῆναι).

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Two forms, one label** | ‑σθαι is middle, ‑θῆναι is passive. The tag "middle/passive" is a statement about the annotation, not about Greek. |
| **2. No augment** | ἐδόμην and ἐδόθην are indicative; the infinitives have no ἐ‑. |
| **3. δυνηθῆναι is not passive in meaning** | "To be able", never "to be enabled". Check whether the verb has an active voice before translating. |
| **4. Present vs. aorist** | δίδοσθαι (present, reduplicated) vs. δόσθαι (aorist). δύνασθαι (present) vs. δυνηθῆναι (aorist). |
| **5. ἵστημι is transitive in the middle** | στήσασθαι "to set up for oneself" is sigmatic; the intransitive aorist is active στῆναι "to stand". |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἐβούλετο τὴν οἰκίαν ἀποδόσθαι.** — "He wanted to sell the house." (middle)
- **ἐκέλευσε τὰ χρήματα ἀποδοθῆναι.** — "He ordered the money to be given back." (passive)
- **οὐκ ἔφη δυνηθῆναι ἐλθεῖν.** — "He said he had not been able to come."
- **ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς συνθέσθαι.** — "They resolved to come to terms."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Complementary infinitive** after βούλομαι, κελεύω, δοκεῖ and ἔφη — the great majority of the tokens in this lesson are of that kind.
2. **δυνηθῆναι** in indirect statement is extremely common: "he said he could/could not".
3. **Reading tip.** Short stem + σθαι means middle; short stem + θῆναι means passive form. Everything else about the two is the same.
