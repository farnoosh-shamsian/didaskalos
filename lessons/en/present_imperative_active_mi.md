# The Present Imperative Active (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, εἰμί)*

---

## 1. What the Present Active Imperative Is

A command, in the voice where the subject does the acting: δίδου "give", τίθει "put", ἴσθι "be".

The present imperative commands an action **as a process** — one that is to continue, be repeated, or is already under way. For a single, self-contained order Greek uses the aorist imperative instead:

| | Form | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | **δίδου** | "keep giving", "be generous" |
| Aorist | δός | "give it" — one act, now |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Endings

Athematic verbs take **‑θι** or a contracted vowel in the 2nd singular, and the ordinary imperative endings elsewhere:

| Person | Ending | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | ‑θι, or contracted ‑ου / ‑ει / ‑η | varies by stem |
| 3 sg | ‑τω | |
| 2 pl | ‑τε | |
| 3 pl | ‑ντων | later also ‑τωσαν |

### 2.2 The Four Main Verbs

| Person | δίδωμι "give" | τίθημι "place" | ἵστημι "make stand" | δείκνυμι "show" |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **δίδου** | **τίθει** | **ἵστη** | **δείκνυ** |
| 3 sg | **διδότω** | **τιθέτω** | **ἱστάτω** | **δεικνύτω** |
| 2 pl | **δίδοτε** | **τίθετε** | **ἵστατε** | **δείκνυτε** |
| 3 pl | **διδόντων** | **τιθέντων** | **ἱστάντων** | **δεικνύντων** |

The 2nd singular forms δίδου, τίθει, ἵστη are contractions of the stem vowel with the old ending ‑ε; they must simply be learned.

### 2.3 The ‑θι Type

Some very common athematic verbs keep the original ending intact:

| Verb | 2 sg imperative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| εἰμί "be" | **ἴσθι** | be! |
| εἶμι "go" | **ἴθι** | go! |
| φημί "say" | **φάθι** | say! |
| οἶδα "know" | **ἴσθι** | know! |

Note that ἴσθι is ambiguous: "be!" from εἰμί and "know!" from οἶδα are spelled alike, and only context tells them apart.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Short stem vowel outside the 2 sg** | διδό‑τω, τιθέ‑τω, ἱστά‑τω. The long ω and η of δίδωμι, τίθημι appear only in the singular indicative. |
| **2. No augment** | An imperative never has one. ἐδίδου (imperfect indicative, "he was giving") and δίδου (imperative, "give!") differ by exactly that ἐ. |
| **3. Prohibitions use μή + present imperative** | μὴ δίδου "don't keep giving". To forbid a single act, use μή + aorist subjunctive: μὴ δῷς "don't give". |
| **4. Compounds pull the accent back** | ἀποδίδου, ἐπιτίθει, παρίστη — the accent recedes as far as the preposition allows. |
| **5. Aspect, not politeness** | Choosing the present over the aorist says something about the shape of the action, not about how courteous the speaker is being. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **δίδου τοῖς πένησιν.** — "Give to the poor." (as a habit)
- **τίθει τὰ ὅπλα.** — "Ground your arms."
- **ἴσθι ἀνὴρ ἀγαθός.** — "Be a good man."
- **ἴθι δὴ καὶ σκόπει.** — "Come now and consider."
- **δείκνυτε ἡμῖν τὴν ὁδόν.** — "Show us the road."
- **μὴ δίδου ὀργῇ τὸν λόγον.** — "Do not keep surrendering your speech to anger."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Plato's dialogues** are full of these: ἴθι δή, φάθι, σκόπει — the conversational imperatives that keep a Socratic argument moving.
2. **ἴσθι in both senses.** ἴσθι σοφός "be wise" and ἴσθι ὅτι… "know that…". The construction that follows usually decides which verb you are looking at.
3. **Reading tip.** A μι-verb imperative is short and endingless-looking: δίδου, τίθει, ἵστη, ἴθι. If a form seems too small to be a verb, an imperative is a good first guess.
