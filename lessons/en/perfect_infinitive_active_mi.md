# The Perfect Active Infinitive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι)*

---

## 1. What This Infinitive Is

"To have given", "to stand" — the action named as complete, with its result in force.

Two shapes belong here, and the second is the one you will meet most:

| Verb | Infinitive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι | **δεδωκέναι** | to have given |
| τίθημι | **τεθεικέναι** | to have placed |
| ἵστημι | **ἑστάναι** | **to stand** |

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Regular Type: ‑κέναι

**reduplication + long stem + ‑κέναι**, with the accent fixed on the ‑έ‑:

- δέδωκα → **δεδωκέναι**
- τέθεικα → **τεθεικέναι**
- ἀποδέδωκα → **ἀποδεδωκέναι**

### 2.2 ἑστάναι: the Athematic Perfect

ἵστημι has a shortened perfect alongside the regular one, and its infinitive belongs to that set:

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ἑστάναι** (also ἑστηκέναι) | to stand |
| Participle **ἑστώς, ἑστῶσα, ἑστός** | standing |

ἑστάναι is far commoner than ἑστηκέναι in Attic prose. Because ἕστηκα means "I stand", the infinitive is a plain present in force: "to be standing".

### 2.3 Others of the Same Kind

| Verb | Perfect infinitive | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| θνῄσκω | **τεθνάναι** (τεθνηκέναι) | to be dead |
| οἶδα | **εἰδέναι** | to know |
| δέδοικα | **δεδιέναι** | to fear |

These are the perfects with present meaning; their infinitives translate as presents.

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. The accent never moves** | δεδωκ**έ**ναι, ἑστ**ά**ναι, εἰδ**έ**ναι: always on the syllable before ‑ναι. |
| **2. No augment** | The perfect and its infinitive never take one; only the pluperfect does. |
| **3. ἑστάναι means "to stand"** | Not "to have stood". The same holds for τεθνάναι "to be dead" and εἰδέναι "to know". |
| **4. Distinguish from the aorist** | δοῦναι "to give" (aorist) vs. δεδωκέναι "to have given" (perfect). One reports the act, the other the state that followed it. |
| **5. Indirect statement keeps the tense** | φησὶ δεδωκέναι "he says he has given"; the perfect infinitive reports a perfect. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ὁμολογεῖ τὰ χρήματα δεδωκέναι.** — "He admits he has given the money."
- **φασὶ τὸν ἄνδρα τεθνάναι.** — "They say the man is dead."
- **λέγεται ὁ ἀνδριὰς ἐν τῇ ἀγορᾷ ἑστάναι.** — "The statue is said to stand in the marketplace."
- **δεῖ τοὺς νόμους καλῶς τεθεικέναι.** — "The laws must have been well established."
- **οὐδεὶς ὁμολογεῖ μηδὲν εἰδέναι.** — "No one admits to knowing nothing."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Indirect statement** after φημί, ὁμολογέω and λέγεται is where nearly all these forms appear.
2. **ἑστάναι and τεθνάναι** are worth learning as vocabulary — they are commoner than most regular perfect infinitives in the language.
3. **Reading tip.** Reduplication + ‑κέναι, or the short athematic ‑άναι: both are perfect active infinitives, and the accent sits just before ‑ναι in either case.
