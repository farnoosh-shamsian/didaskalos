# The Perfect Middle/Passive Participle (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, δείκνυμι)*

---

## 1. What This Participle Is

The perfect expresses a completed action **whose result still stands**. Its middle/passive participle therefore describes something *in the resulting state*: δεδομένος is not "being given" but "**having been given**, now in someone's hands."

μι-verbs form it exactly as ω-verbs do — the difference is the stem, not the ending. Because μι-verbs are athematic, the ending attaches straight to the perfect stem with no linking vowel.

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **δεδομένος** | having been given / given |
| **τεθειμένος** | having been placed / set |
| **ἑσταμένος** | standing (result of "having been made to stand") |

Middle force is also possible where the verb has one: "having done for oneself."

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**reduplicated perfect stem + ‑μένος, ‑μένη, ‑μένον**

| Verb | Perfect middle/passive | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| δίδωμι "give" | δέδομαι | **δεδομένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| τίθημι "place" | τέθειμαι | **τεθειμένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| ἵημι "send" | (ἀφ)εῖμαι | **(ἀφ)ειμένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| ἵστημι "make stand" | ἕσταμαι | **ἑσταμένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| δείκνυμι "show" | δέδειγμαι | **δεδειγμένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |
| δύναμαι "be able" | δεδύνημαι | **δεδυνημένος, ‑η, ‑ον** |

### 2.2 Declension: **δεδομένος** "having been given"

It declines like any first/second declension adjective (ἀγαθός, ‑ή, ‑όν).

| Case | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Neut. sg. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. | Neut. pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | δεδομένος | δεδομένη | δεδομένον | δεδομένοι | δεδομέναι | δεδομένα |
| Gen. | δεδομένου | δεδομένης | δεδομένου | δεδομένων | δεδομένων | δεδομένων |
| Dat. | δεδομένῳ | δεδομένῃ | δεδομένῳ | δεδομένοις | δεδομέναις | δεδομένοις |
| Acc. | δεδομένον | δεδομένην | δεδομένον | δεδομένους | δεδομένας | δεδομένα |

### 2.3 Consonant Stems

When the stem ends in a consonant it meets the **μ** of the ending and assimilates, exactly as in the perfect indicative middle/passive:

- δείκνυμι: δεικ‑ + μένος → **δεδειγμένος** (κ → γ before μ)
- ζεύγνυμι: ζευγ‑ + μένος → **ἐζευγμένος**

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. The accent never moves** | ‑μένος is always accented on that syllable: δεδομένος, δεδομένου, δεδομένων. Unlike most participles it is not recessive, and the accent does not shift in the genitive plural. |
| **2. Reduplication is the tense marker** | Compare present **διδόμενος** "being given" with perfect **δεδομένος** "having been given". Only the reduplication distinguishes them, and the difference in meaning is large. |
| **3. No thematic vowel** | ω-verbs show λελυ‑**μένος**; μι-verbs show δεδο‑**μένος** — the same ending on a shorter stem. There is nothing extra to learn. |
| **4. Middle or passive is context** | δεδομένος is "given" (passive) far more often than "having given for oneself" (middle). The sentence decides. |
| **5. Periphrastic forms** | With εἰμί it makes a compound tense: δεδομένον ἐστί "it has been given", δεδομένα ἦν "they had been given". |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **τὰ δεδομένα** — "the things given" (in mathematics, "the data").
- **ὁ νόμος ὑπὸ τῶν προγόνων τεθειμένος** — "the law laid down by our ancestors."
- **τὰ ὅπλα τοῖς πολεμίοις δεδομένα ἦν.** — "The weapons had been handed over to the enemy."
- **οἱ ἐν τῇ ἀγορᾷ ἑσταμένοι** — "those standing in the marketplace."
- **τὰ δεδειγμένα ὑπὸ τοῦ διδασκάλου** — "the things demonstrated by the teacher."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Attributive use is everywhere.** With the article the participle becomes a noun phrase — τὰ δεδομένα, οἱ τεταγμένοι "those posted (on duty)" — and this is how you will most often meet it in prose.
2. **State, not event.** Where an aorist participle (δούς "having given") reports the act, the perfect participle reports the situation it produced. Its force is usually adjectival: "given", "established", "appointed".
3. **ἑσταμένος and ἑστώς.** ἵστημι has both a middle/passive participle ἑσταμένος and an active ἑστώς, ἑστῶσα, ἑστός; both mean "standing", and the active form is the commoner in Attic prose.
