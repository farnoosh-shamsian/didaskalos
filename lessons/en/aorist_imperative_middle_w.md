# The Aorist Imperative Middle (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, λαμβάνω, γίγνομαι)*

---

## 1. What the Aorist Middle Imperative Is

An imperative gives a command. The **middle** voice makes the subject act on or for itself: λῦσαι σεαυτόν "free yourself", παῦσαι "stop (yourself)".

The choice between present and aorist imperative is **aspect**, not time — an aorist imperative does not command something in the past:

| | Form | Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | λύου | "keep loosing", be in the process of it |
| **Aorist** | **λῦσαι** | "loose", just do it — one complete act |

The aorist is the ordinary form for a single, definite order; the present suits a standing rule or an action already under way.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Sigmatic Aorist Middle: **λύω** → **λῦσαι** "free (for yourself)"

**aorist stem + ‑σα‑ + middle imperative endings** (no augment — the augment belongs to the indicative)

| Person | Ending | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | ‑σαι | **λῦσαι** | free! (you) |
| 3 sg | ‑σάσθω | **λυσάσθω** | let him/her free |
| 2 pl | ‑σασθε | **λύσασθε** | free! (you pl.) |
| 3 pl | ‑σάσθων | **λυσάσθων** | let them free |

Later Greek also uses ‑σάσθωσαν for the 3rd plural.

### 2.2 Thematic Aorist Middle: **λαμβάνω** → **λαβοῦ** "take"

Thematic aorists use the thematic endings on the bare aorist stem:

| Person | Ending | λαμβάνω | γίγνομαι |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | ‑οῦ | **λαβοῦ** | **γενοῦ** |
| 3 sg | ‑έσθω | **λαβέσθω** | **γενέσθω** |
| 2 pl | ‑εσθε | **λάβεσθε** | **γένεσθε** |
| 3 pl | ‑έσθων | **λαβέσθων** | **γενέσθων** |

Note the 2 sg accent: **λαβοῦ, γενοῦ, ἑλοῦ** carry a circumflex on the ending. The accent does not retract in compounds: λαβοῦ → **ἀπολαβοῦ**. Contrast the active, where it does: λαβέ → **ἀπόλαβε**.

### 2.3 Prohibitions

A negative command in the aorist does **not** use the imperative. Use μή + aorist subjunctive:

- **μὴ λύσῃ** — "don't free" (a single act)
- but **μὴ λύου** — "stop freeing / don't be freeing" (present imperative, ongoing)

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐλυσάμην is indicative; the imperative is λῦσαι. An augmented imperative does not exist. |
| **2. λῦσαι is a famous three-way ambiguity** | **λῦσαι** = aorist middle imperative "free!"; **λῦσαι** = aorist active infinitive "to free"; **λύσαι** = aorist active optative 3 sg "may he free". Accent and context separate them: the infinitive and imperative differ only by context, the optative by its accent. |
| **3. Middle, not passive** | The aorist passive imperative is different: λύθητι "be freed". Do not read λῦσαι as a passive. |
| **4. Aspect, not tense** | Both λύου and λῦσαι command something now. The aorist sees the act as a whole; the present sees it as continuing. |
| **5. Deponents use these endings normally** | γίγνομαι, ἀποκρίνομαι and other middle-only verbs form ordinary aorist middle imperatives: γενοῦ, ἀποκρίναι. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **παῦσαι λέγων.** — "Stop talking."
- **γενοῦ ἀνὴρ ἀγαθός.** — "Be a good man." (become one)
- **λαβοῦ τῆς χειρός μου.** — "Take hold of my hand."
- **φύλαξαι τοὺς κόλακας.** — "Be on your guard against flatterers."
- **δέξασθε τοὺς ἱκέτας.** — "Receive the suppliants."
- **μὴ φοβηθῇς τὸν θάνατον.** — "Do not fear death." (prohibition: μή + aorist subjunctive)

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Prose and drama.** Single sharp orders — παῦσαι, φύλαξαι, δέξαι — are the aorist middle's home; Attic tragedy and the orators are full of them.
2. **Politeness is not the issue.** Greek does not soften a command by choosing the present; that is done with particles (ἄγε, δή) or with an optative of wish.
3. **Learn the 2 sg forms as vocabulary.** λῦσαι, παῦσαι, γενοῦ, λαβοῦ, δέξαι, ἑλοῦ: these six shapes cover most of what you will meet.
