# The Imperfect Indicative Middle (ω-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Imperfect Indicative Middle Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Imperfect** | The past of process or repetition: "was doing," "used to do." |
| **Indicative** | The "statement" mood — the imperfect exists only in the indicative. |
| **Middle** | The subject acts on or for itself — or the verb is a deponent with active meaning. |
| **(w)** | The thematic verbs in ‑ω (λύω) and the deponents in ‑ομαι. |

In the imperfect, middle and passive share the same forms; this lesson gathers the tokens annotated specifically as **middle** — self‑involved action, or a deponent.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Construction: Augment + Present Stem + Secondary Middle Endings

| Person | Ending | λύω → | Meaning (middle) |
|--------|--------|-------|------------------|
| 1st sg. | **‑όμην** | **ἐλυόμην** | I was ransoming |
| 2nd sg. | **‑ου** (from ‑ε‑σο) | **ἐλύου** | you were… |
| 3rd sg. | **‑ετο** | **ἐλύετο** | he/she/it was… |
| 1st pl. | **‑όμεθα** | **ἐλυόμεθα** | we were… |
| 2nd pl. | **‑εσθε** | **ἐλύεσθε** | you (pl.) were… |
| 3rd pl. | **‑οντο** | **ἐλύοντο** | they were… |

*The 2nd singular: ἐλύε‑σο → ἐλύε‑ο → ἐλύου (σ dropped, ε + ο contracted).*

### 2.2 Common Deponent Imperfects

| Verb | Imperfect (1st/3rd sg.) | Meaning |
|------|-------------------------|---------|
| **ἔρχομαι** | **ἠρχόμην / ἤρχετο** | I was coming / he was coming |
| **γίγνομαι** | **ἐγιγνόμην / ἐγίγνετο** | was becoming, kept happening |
| **βούλομαι** | **ἐβουλόμην / ἐβούλετο** | wanted |
| **μάχομαι** | **ἐμαχόμην / ἐμάχετο** | was fighting |
| **πορεύομαι** | **ἐπορευόμην / ἐπορεύετο** | was traveling |
| **ἡγέομαι** | **ἡγούμην / ἡγεῖτο** | was leading (contract) |

### 2.3 The Augment

The same rules as in the active: syllabic ἐ‑ before consonants (ἐ‑λυόμην), temporal lengthening before vowels (ἡγούμην from ἡγέομαι).

---

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. Augment marks past time** | Only the indicative takes it. | ἐγίγνετο |
| **2. Process or repetition** | Contrast the aorist's single act. | ἐμάχοντο "they kept fighting" vs. ἐμαχέσαντο |
| **3. Deponents translate actively** | ἐβούλετο "he wanted," never passive. | — |
| **4. ἤρχετο ambiguity** | Can be from ἔρχομαι "was coming" or ἄρχομαι "was beginning" — context decides. | — |
| **5. Contract deponents contract as usual** | ε + ο → ου etc. | ἡγεῖτο, ἐφοβεῖτο |
| **6. Inceptive imperfect** | Often "began to…" in narrative. | ἐπορεύετο "he set out / was on his way" |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **ἤρχετο πρὸς τὴν θάλατταν.** | He was going toward the sea. (deponent) |
| **ἐβούλοντο εἰρήνην ἄγειν.** | They wanted to keep peace. (deponent) |
| **ταῦτα ἐγίγνετο πολλάκις.** | These things kept happening often. (deponent) |
| **οἱ στρατιῶται ἐμάχοντο πᾶσαν τὴν ἡμέραν.** | The soldiers were fighting all day. (deponent) |
| **ἐλύετο τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους.** | He was ransoming the prisoners. (true middle) |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **The imperfect is the backbone of Greek narrative**, painting the ongoing background against which aorists report events — and deponent imperfects (ἤρχετο, ἐγίγνετο, ἐβούλετο) carry much of that background.
2. **Repeated or customary past action** ("used to…") regularly takes the imperfect.
3. **Conative force**: sometimes "tried to…" — ἐπείθετο can shade into "he was trying to comply."
