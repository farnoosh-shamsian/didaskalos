# The Present Optative Middle/Passive (μι-Verbs)
*(e.g., δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, δύναμαι)*

---

## 1. What This Optative Is

The optative expresses a wish, a possibility, or — most often in prose — what someone said or intended, reported in a past context. The present stem marks the action as **ongoing**, not as a single event.

| Form | Force |
| --- | --- |
| **διδοῖτο** | may it be given / that it might be being given |
| **δύναιτο** | he might be able |
| **ἱσταῖτο** | he might stand |

δύναμαι and ἐπίσταμαι are deponent, so this is the *only* optative they have — and δύναιτο is by a wide margin the commonest form in the whole lesson.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**present stem + the optative marker ‑ι‑ + secondary middle endings.** The marker fuses with the stem vowel: ο + ι → οι, ε + ι → ει, α + ι → αι.

### 2.2 Paradigms

| Person | δίδωμι | τίθημι | ἵστημι | δύναμαι |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **διδοίμην** | **τιθείμην** | **ἱσταίμην** | **δυναίμην** |
| 2 sg | **διδοῖο** | **τιθεῖο** | **ἱσταῖο** | **δύναιο** |
| 3 sg | **διδοῖτο** | **τιθεῖτο** | **ἱσταῖτο** | **δύναιτο** |
| 1 pl | **διδοίμεθα** | **τιθείμεθα** | **ἱσταίμεθα** | **δυναίμεθα** |
| 2 pl | **διδοῖσθε** | **τιθεῖσθε** | **ἱσταῖσθε** | **δύναισθε** |
| 3 pl | **διδοῖντο** | **τιθεῖντο** | **ἱσταῖντο** | **δύναιντο** |

### 2.3 The Diphthong Is the Marker

οι, ει, αι in the ending is what makes the form optative. Compare:

| Mood | δίδωμι 3 sg |
| --- | --- |
| Indicative | δίδοται |
| Subjunctive | διδῶται |
| **Optative** | **διδοῖτο** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | The optative never takes one, in any tense. |
| **2. Secondary endings on a present stem** | ‑μην, ‑ο, ‑το: the same endings as the imperfect, but with no augment and with the ι‑diphthong before them. |
| **3. δύναιτο ἄν is the potential** | With ἄν the optative means "could, might": οὐκ ἂν δύναιτο "he would not be able". Without ἄν it is a wish or a reported thought. |
| **4. Secondary sequence** | After a past main verb, a present subjunctive of direct speech becomes this optative: ἐβούλετο ἵνα διδοῖτο. |
| **5. Present, not aorist** | διδοῖτο (present, ongoing) vs. δοῖτο (aorist, single act). The reduplicating δι‑ marks the present. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **οὐκ ἂν δύναιτο ταῦτα ποιεῖν.** — "He would not be able to do this."
- **εἴθε δύναισθε ἡμῖν βοηθῆσαι.** — "If only you could help us!"
- **ἐβουλεύοντο ὅπως ἡ χώρα μὴ τοῖς πολεμίοις διδοῖτο.** — "They were deliberating so that the land might not be handed over to the enemy."
- **οὐκ ἐπίσταιτο ἄν τις ταῦτα ἄνευ διδασκάλου.** — "No one could know this without a teacher."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **δύναιτο ἄν and δύναιντο ἄν** are the everyday way of saying "could" in Attic prose; learn them as vocabulary.
2. **Purpose clauses in past narrative** take this optative after ἵνα, ὅπως and ὡς.
3. **Reading tip.** A middle ending with οι/ει/αι in front of it and no augment is an optative. If the stem is reduplicated (διδο‑, τιθε‑, ἱστα‑), it is present.
