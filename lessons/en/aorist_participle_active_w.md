# The Aorist Active Participle (ω-Verbs)

---

## 1. What Is the Aorist Active Participle?

The **aorist active participle** is a non-finite form that:

* Presents the action as a **complete event** (aorist aspect), not one in progress (present).
* **Behaves like an adjective**: it agrees with its noun in gender, number, and case.
* **Has active voice**: the noun it modifies is the doer of the action.

It usually reports the action as a complete whole and **prior to** the action of the main verb — "having …ed," "after …ing," or a relative clause will all carry that:

* *ὁ γράψας ἀνήρ* — "the man **who wrote**"
* *τὰ πεσόντα δένδρα* — "the trees **that fell**"

The uses of the participle fall under three headings:

* **Supplementary** — the participle is an **obligatory** part of the clause: it completes a verb that would be incomplete without it. This is the participle that goes with verbs of perceiving, knowing and showing, and with verbs such as τυγχάνω and λανθάνω.
* **Circumstantial** — the participle is an **optional** addition to the clause, supplying a circumstance: time, cause, condition, concession, motivation, purpose. Either it agrees with a word already in the clause (the **connected** use), or it is added with a subject of its own in the genitive (the **genitive absolute**).
* **Attributive and substantival** — with the article, the participle sits inside a noun phrase, either modifying a noun (**attributive**: *τὰ πεσόντα δένδρα*) or standing on its own as the head of the phrase (**substantival**: *ὁ γράψας* "the man who wrote").

> **A note on "predicative."** In Greek grammar *predicative* names a **position**, not a use. Supplementary and circumstantial participles both stand in predicative position with respect to any noun they agree with; attributive participles stand in attributive position. εἰμί with a participle is a separate matter again — a periphrastic construction, not one of the three uses above.

## 2. Formation (Paradigm)

### 2.1 Basic Morphology

Sigmatic aorist participle = **verb stem + ‑σα‑ + the suffix ‑ντ‑ + endings** (no augment):

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---------------------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Singular (nom.)** | **‑σας** | **‑σασα** | **‑σαν** |
| **Plural (nom.)** | **‑σαντες** | **‑σασαι** | **‑σαντα** |

> **Note:** the endings ‑ων/‑ουσα/‑ον belong to the **present** participle (and the thematic aorist); the sigmatic aorist always has ‑ας/‑ασα/‑αν.

### 2.2 Full Declension Table (example verb: **γράφω** "to write")

| Case | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Neut. sg. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. | Neut. pl. |
|---------------|--------------------|-------------------|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Nominative** | γράψας | γράψασα | γράψαν | γράψαντες | γράψασαι | γράψαντα |
| **Genitive** | γράψαντος | γραψάσης | γράψαντος | γραψάντων | γραψασῶν | γραψάντων |
| **Dative** | γράψαντι | γραψάσῃ | γράψαντι | γράψασι(ν) | γραψάσαις | γράψασι(ν) |
| **Accusative** | γράψαντα | γράψασαν | γράψαν | γράψαντας | γραψάσας | γράψαντα |

> Masculine and neuter follow the third declension (ντ-stem), the feminine the first declension. The vocative is rare and identical to the nominative.

### 2.3 Other Types

| Verb type | Aorist | Participle (masc. nom.) |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Regular** | ἔγραψα | **γράψας** |
| **Contract** (lengthened vowel) | ἐφίλησα, ἐτίμησα | **φιλήσας**, **τιμήσας** |
| **Liquid/nasal stem** | ἤγγειλα, ἔφηνα, ἔμεινα | **ἀγγείλας**, **φήνας**, **μείνας** |
| **Thematic aorist** (endings in ‑ών) | ἔλαβον, εἶδον, ἦλθον | **λαβών**, **ἰδών**, **ἐλθών** (genitive: λαβόντος) |
| **μι-Verbs** | ἔδωκα, ἔθηκα | **δούς**, **θείς** (in the μι lesson) |

> **Liquid and nasal stems.** In verbs whose stem ends in λ, μ, ν or ρ the σ has dropped out and the vowel before it has lengthened in compensation — the **pseudo-sigmatic** aorist. There is no σ to see, but the endings are the ordinary ‑ας/‑ασα/‑αν.

> **Practical tip:** for most regular verbs the pattern "stem + σας" works without trouble; for irregular verbs, form the participle from the third principal part (the aorist indicative) by dropping the augment: ἔλαβον → λαβών.

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| **Agreement** | The participle agrees with its noun in **gender, number, and case**. |
| **Aspect** | It conveys a **single, complete** action, usually **prior to** the main verb. |
| **No augment** | ἔγραψα but γράψας — the augment belongs to the indicative alone. |
| **Position** | With the article = attributive/relative (ὁ γράψας); without the article = circumstantial. |
| **With verbs of perceiving and knowing** | Here the participle completes the verb. With verbs of **direct** perception the participle is nearly always present; an aorist marks the event as perceived in its entirety, and is still simultaneous, not prior: *ὡς δὲ εἶδεν ἔλαφον ἐκπηδήσασαν* "when he saw a deer spring out" (Xen. *Cyr.* 1.4.8). With ἀκούω and αἰσθάνομαι of direct perception the participle and its subject stand in the **genitive**: *ἤκουσαν αὐτοῦ φωνήσαντος* "they heard him speak." |
| **Temporal and causal** | "when/after he wrote" or "since he wrote" — context decides; particles such as ἅτε (cause) and καίπερ (concession) make the sense explicit. |
| **Thematic aorist** | Endings ‑ών/‑οῦσα/‑όν with the accent on the ending; distinguished from the present participle by the stem: λαμβάνων (present) ≠ λαβών (aorist). |

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|------------------------------|---------------------|
| **ὁ ἀνὴρ γράψας τὸν λόγον ἀπῆλθεν.** | The man who wrote the speech left. |
| **ἡ γυνὴ παιδεύσασα τὸν παῖδα εὐδαίμων ἦν.** | The woman who educated the child was happy. |
| **λύσας τὸν δεσμόν, ἀπῆλθον.** | Having loosed the bond, I went away. |
| **ἰδὼν τὸν ἐχθρόν, ὁ στρατηγὸς ἐβόησεν.** | When he saw the enemy, the general shouted. |
| **ἀκούσας τὸν λόγον, ἔγραψε τὴν ἀπόκρισιν.** | Having heard the speech, he wrote the reply. |

## 5. Usage & Contextual Notes

1. **Temporal sequence** — the aorist participle usually reports an action **prior to** the main verb: *ἀκούσας ταῦτα, ἀπῆλθεν* "having heard this, he left."
2. **But not always prior** — the aorist participle is not infrequently used of an action that does **not** precede the main verb but **coincides** with it (the **coincident** aorist participle); the aorist then presents the action as a single whole. It is especially common with circumstantial participles of manner: *δοκεῖ μοί τις οὐκ ἂν ἁμαρτεῖν εἰπὼν ὅτι…* "it seems to me that one would not be mistaken in saying that…" (Dem. 25.6) — the saying and the being-mistaken are one and the same moment.
3. **Causal meaning** — "because…": *γνοὺς τὸν κίνδυνον, ἔφυγεν* "realizing the danger, he fled"; ἅτε + participle makes the cause explicit.
4. **Condition and concession** — without the article it can be conditional ("if…") or, with καίπερ, concessive ("although…"): *καίπερ γράψας* "although he wrote."
5. **The genitive absolute** — when the participle's subject is not part of the main clause, the participle and its subject both stand in the genitive: *Πεισιστράτου τελευτήσαντος Ἱππίας ἔσχε τὴν ἀρχήν* "when Peisistratus died, Hippias succeeded to his power" (Thuc. 6.54) — a pattern you will meet in the syntax lessons.
6. **Frequency** — the aorist participle is among the most frequent constructions of Greek prose; chains of "participle + main verb" are the backbone of narrative.

---

## 6. Historical Note

The suffix ‑ντ‑ (visible in γράψαντος) is the ancient active participle suffix; combined with the sigmatic marker ‑σα‑ it produced ‑σας (from *‑σαντς). The feminine ‑σασα arose from *‑σαντ-ya by regular sound change.
