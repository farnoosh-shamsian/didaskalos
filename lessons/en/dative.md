# The Dative Case

The **dative** is the case of the person or thing concerned. Its central job is the indirect object — the one to whom or for whom something is done — but it also names the means by which an action is carried out, the manner in which, the time at which, and the person who has something. It is the case that follows ἐν, σύν and a good many verbs.

Its range looks wide because three older cases lie behind it. Indo-European had a separate **dative** ("to, for"), **instrumental** ("by means of, together with") and **locative** ("in, at"); Greek collapsed all three into one form. Sorting a dative into one of those three ideas — *to/for*, *by/with*, *in/at* — is usually the quickest way to translate it.

---

## 1. Indirect Object

With verbs of giving, saying, showing and the like, the recipient stands in the dative while the thing given stands in the accusative.

- **δῶρα τοῖς θεοῖς φέρομεν.** — "We bring gifts to the gods."
- **λέγε αὐτοῖς τοὺς νόμους.** — "Read them the laws."

---

## 2. Dative of Interest

Wider than the indirect object: the dative names the person **for whom**, or in whose eyes, something is true.

- **τοῦτο ἡμῖν συμφέρει.** — "This is to our advantage."
- **δοκεῖ μοι.** — "It seems to me / I think so."

**Dative of possession.** With εἰμί the dative names the owner, and Greek says "there is X to Y" where English says "Y has X":

- **ἔστι μοι βιβλίον.** — "I have a book."
- **οὐδὲν ἡμῖν ἐστιν ἀγαθόν.** — "We have nothing good."

---

## 3. Means and Instrument

The bare dative — no preposition — names the thing **by means of which** something is done. This is the old instrumental.

- **τῇ μαχαίρᾳ ἔπαισεν.** — "He struck with the sword."
- **τοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ὁρῶμεν.** — "We see with our eyes."

Take care: a person is normally **not** put in this dative. The agent of a passive verb is ὑπό + genitive; the bare dative is for the instrument, not the doer.

Related and very common is the **dative of degree of difference** with comparatives: **πολλῷ μείζων** "greater by much", **ὀλίγῳ πρότερον** "a little earlier".

---

## 4. Manner, Respect and Cause

- **Manner** — the dative answers "how?": **σιγῇ** "in silence", **δρόμῳ** "at a run", **τούτῳ τῷ τρόπῳ** "in this way", **τῷ λόγῳ** "in argument, in theory".
- **Respect** — it names the point in which something holds: **δυνάμει διαφέρει** "he differs **in power**", **γένει Ἀθηναῖος** "an Athenian **by birth**".
- **Cause** — it names the reason or the feeling that prompts: **φόβῳ ἔφυγον** "they fled **out of fear**."

---

## 5. Time When

The dative marks the **point** at which something happens — as against the genitive (within which) and the accusative (for how long).

- **ταύτῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ** "on that day" · **τῇ ὑστεραίᾳ** "on the next day"
- Compare: **τριῶν ἡμερῶν** "within three days" (genitive) · **τρεῖς ἡμέρας** "for three days" (accusative)

---

## 6. After Prepositions

Only a few prepositions govern the dative, but they are among the commonest words in Greek:

- **ἐν** + dat. "in, among": ἐν τῇ πόλει "in the city"
- **σύν** + dat. "together with": σὺν τοῖς φίλοις "with his friends"
- **ἐπί, παρά, πρός** + dat. for rest or nearness: ἐπὶ τῇ θαλάττῃ "by the sea", παρὰ τῷ βασιλεῖ "at the king's court", πρὸς τούτοις "in addition to this"
- **ὑπό** + dat. "under" (at rest): ὑπὸ τῷ δένδρῳ "under the tree"

The dative is the "where" case in the pattern the module *Prepositions* sets out: accusative for whither, dative for where, genitive for whence.

---

## 7. Verbs Governing the Dative

Many Greek verbs take a dative where English has a plain object. Learn the case with the verb:

| Meaning | Verbs |
| --- | --- |
| obeying, trusting, following | πείθομαι, πιστεύω, ἕπομαι, ἀκολουθέω |
| helping, being useful | βοηθέω, συμφέρω |
| meeting, associating, quarrelling | ἐντυγχάνω, χράομαι, διαφέρομαι |
| being angry, envying, blaming | ὀργίζομαι, φθονέω, μέμφομαι |
| being like, being friendly | ἔοικα, φίλος εἰμί |

**χράομαι** "use" is worth memorizing on its own — it takes the dative of the thing used: τῷ λόγῳ χρῆσθαι "to use the argument". Compounds with σύν, ἐν and ἐπί also tend to take a dative.

---

## 8. The Endings, Declension by Declension

One thread runs through the whole system: the dative singular is marked by an **iota** (written subscript after a long vowel, on the line after a consonant stem), and the dative plural by a **sigma**.

### 1st Declension (‑ᾱ/‑ᾰ/‑η stems)

| Type | Nominative | Dative sg. | Dative pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feminine in ‑η | ἡ τιμή | τῇ τιμῇ | ταῖς τιμαῖς |
| Feminine in ‑ᾱ | ἡ χώρα | τῇ χώρᾳ | ταῖς χώραις |
| Feminine in ‑ᾰ | ἡ θάλαττα | τῇ θαλάττῃ | ταῖς θαλάτταις |
| Masculine in ‑ης | ὁ πολίτης | τῷ πολίτῃ | τοῖς πολίταις |
| Masculine in ‑ᾱς | ὁ νεανίας | τῷ νεανίᾳ | τοῖς νεανίαις |

_Key point_ — the dative singular follows the **genitive**, not the nominative: nouns whose genitive is in ‑ᾱς have **‑ᾳ** (χώρα → χώρας → χώρᾳ), and nouns whose genitive is in ‑ης have **‑ῃ**. So short-alpha nouns like θάλαττα and δόξα take ‑ῃ (θαλάττῃ, δόξῃ), not ‑ᾳ. The plural is **‑αις** throughout.

### 2nd Declension (‑ο stems)

| Gender | Nominative | Dative sg. | Dative pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine | ὁ φίλος | τῷ φίλῳ | τοῖς φίλοις |
| Feminine | ἡ ὁδός | τῇ ὁδῷ | ταῖς ὁδοῖς |
| Neuter | τὸ δῶρον | τῷ δώρῳ | τοῖς δώροις |

The ‑ο of the stem lengthens and takes the iota subscript: **‑ῳ**. The plural is **‑οις**.

### 3rd Declension (consonant and vowel stems)

Build these on the stem the **genitive singular** reveals (φύλαξ, φύλακος → φυλακ‑).

| Stem type | Nominative | Dative sg. | Dative pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consonant stem | ὁ φύλαξ (φύλακος) | τῷ φύλακι | τοῖς φύλαξι(ν) |
| ‑ρ stem | ὁ πατήρ (πατρός) | τῷ πατρί | τοῖς πατράσι(ν) |
| Irregular ‑ρ stem | ὁ ἀνήρ (ἀνδρός) | τῷ ἀνδρί | τοῖς ἀνδράσι(ν) |
| Nasal stem | ὁ δαίμων (δαίμονος) | τῷ δαίμονι | τοῖς δαίμοσι(ν) |
| Neuter consonant stem | τὸ σῶμα (σώματος) | τῷ σώματι | τοῖς σώμασι(ν) |
| ι‑stem | ἡ πόλις (πόλεως) | τῇ πόλει | ταῖς πόλεσι(ν) |
| υ‑stem | ὁ ἰχθύς (ἰχθύος) | τῷ ἰχθύι | τοῖς ἰχθύσι(ν) |
| ευ‑stem | ὁ βασιλεύς (βασιλέως) | τῷ βασιλεῖ | τοῖς βασιλεῦσι(ν) |
| σ‑stem neuter | τὸ γένος (γένους) | τῷ γένει | τοῖς γένεσι(ν) |
| Irregular | ἡ ναῦς (νεώς) | τῇ νηΐ | ταῖς ναυσί(ν) |

_Key points_

- The singular ending is **‑ι**, the plural **‑σι(ν)**.
- The stem's final consonant meets that ‑σι and changes: φυλακ‑σι → **φύλαξι**, σωματ‑σι → **σώμασι**, δαιμον‑σι → **δαίμοσι**.
- ι‑, ευ‑ and σ‑stems contract: πόλε(ι) → **πόλει**, βασιλέ(ι) → **βασιλεῖ**, γένε(σ)ι → **γένει**.
- The **movable ν** may be added to ‑σι before a vowel or at a pause: τοῖς ἀνδράσιν ἔλεγεν.

---

## 9. Summary of Endings

| Declension | Dative sg. | Dative pl. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ‑ᾳ / ‑ῃ (following the genitive) | ‑αις |
| 2nd | ‑ῳ | ‑οις |
| 3rd | ‑ι (contracting stems ‑ει) | ‑σι(ν) |

---

## 10. Points to Watch

- The iota subscript is **not** optional and not decorative: τῇ ὁδῷ is a dative, τῆς ὁδοῦ a genitive, and the article is often the clearest signal of which you have.
- ‑οις and ‑αις belong to the dative plural of the first two declensions only; a third-declension plural will end in **‑σι(ν)**.
- A bare dative with no preposition is means, manner, time, interest or possession — run through §2–§5 before assuming it is an indirect object.
- If the verb is one of the families in §7, the dative *is* its object, however English translates it.

---

## 11. Historical Development

Of all the cases, the dative changes most across the span of Greek — in its endings, in its jobs, and finally in whether it exists at all.

**Three cases in one.** The Greek dative does the work of three older cases: a dative proper ("to/for"), an **instrumental** ("by means of, together with") and a **locative** ("in, at"), which fell together into a single form. That is why one case covers such an odd range of meanings. The old locative survives visibly in fossils like **Ἀθήνησι** "at Athens" and **οἴκοι** "at home", and the ‑φι(ν) ending Homer uses for both instrument and place (βίηφι "by force", ναῦφι "at the ships") is another relic of the same system.

**The endings were longer.**

| Case | Homer | Ionic (Herodotus) | Attic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st decl. plural | ‑ῃσι(ν), ‑ῃς (θεῇσι) | ‑ῃσι | ‑αις |
| 2nd decl. plural | ‑οισι(ν) (θεοῖσι) | ‑οισι | ‑οις |
| 3rd decl. plural | ‑σι(ν) and **‑εσσι(ν)** (πόδεσσι) | ‑σι | ‑σι(ν) |

These long datives are among the most conspicuous features of Homeric Greek; if a text is full of ‑οισι and ‑ῃσι, you are reading epic or Ionic.

The heading above says "were longer", and that needs one qualification. Attic wrote ‑ᾱσι and ‑οισι itself until around 420 BCE, so the short endings are a late-fifth-century Athenian development, not an ancient wearing-down. In most other dialects ‑αις and ‑οις are the only forms on record from the start. So the long datives mark epic and Ionic specifically, rather than an older stage of Greek as a whole.

**Then the case recedes.** In Homer and Classical Attic the bare dative does a great deal of work with no preposition at all: **τῇ μαχαίρᾳ** "with the sword", **τῷ λόγῳ** "by the argument", **ταύτῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ** "on that day". Over the Hellenistic period Greek increasingly props the case up with a preposition instead — **ἐν** + dative for instrument and place — and where the dative marked a goal or recipient, **εἰς** or **πρός** + accusative starts to take over. In the New Testament ἐν is extremely frequent, doing work that Plato would have left to a bare dative.

| | Classical Attic | Koine tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument | bare dative: μαχαίρᾳ | ἐν μαχαίρῃ |
| Place where | bare dative or ἐν | almost always ἐν |
| Goal / recipient | dative | dative, but εἰς/πρός + acc. spreading |

So when a Koine text uses a preposition where you expected a plain dative, you are watching the beginning of a long retreat — not sloppiness, but the language in motion.

## 12. Example Sentences

| Greek | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **λέγε αὐτοῖς τοὺς νόμους.** | Read them the laws. *(indirect object)* |
| **ἔστι μοι βιβλίον.** | I have a book. *(possession)* |
| **τῇ μαχαίρᾳ ἔπαισεν.** | He struck with the sword. *(means)* |
| **τούτῳ τῷ τρόπῳ ἐποίησαν.** | They did it in this way. *(manner)* |
| **ταύτῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἀπῆλθον.** | On that day they departed. *(time when)* |
| **ἐν τῇ πόλει μένουσιν.** | They remain in the city. *(after a preposition)* |
| **τῷ λόγῳ χρῆσθαι βούλομαι.** | I wish to use the argument. *(verb governing the dative)* |
