# The Aorist Imperative Middle/Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γίγνομαι, φοβέομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Form Is

A command to do something once and completely, either for oneself or by undergoing it. As always in the aorist, the two voices are separate formations:

| Voice | 2 sg | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Middle | **λῦσαι** | free (for yourself)! |
| Passive | **λύθητι** | be freed! |

A "middle/passive" tag here usually marks a **deponent**, whose middle or ‑θη‑ form is active in meaning: γενοῦ "become!", φοβήθητι "be afraid!".

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Middle

| Person | Sigmatic aorist | Thematic aorist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **λῦσαι** | **λαβοῦ**, **γενοῦ** |
| 3 sg | **λυσάσθω** | **λαβέσθω** |
| 2 pl | **λύσασθε** | **λάβεσθε** |
| 3 pl | **λυσάσθων** | **λαβέσθων** |

### 2.2 Passive

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 2 sg | **λύθητι** |
| 3 sg | **λυθήτω** |
| 2 pl | **λύθητε** |
| 3 pl | **λυθέντων** |

The 2 sg ending is ‑θητι, not ‑θηθι: Greek dissimilates the second of two aspirates.

### 2.3 Deponents

| Verb | Imperative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| γίγνομαι | **γενοῦ** | become! |
| ἀποκρίνομαι | **ἀπόκριναι** | answer! |
| φοβέομαι | **φοβήθητι** | be afraid! |
| πορεύομαι | **πορεύθητι** | go! |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐλυσάμην, ἐλύθην are indicative; the imperatives drop the ἐ‑. |
| **2. λῦσαι is three forms in one** | Aorist middle imperative, aorist active infinitive, and (accented λύσαι) aorist active optative 3 sg. |
| **3. ‑θητι does not always mean passive** | πορεύθητι "go", φοβήθητι "be afraid": deponents use the passive shape with active force. |
| **4. Negative commands** | μή + aorist subjunctive: μὴ φοβηθῇς, μὴ γένῃ. There is no negative aorist imperative. |
| **5. Aspect, not time** | Both present and aorist imperatives command something now; the aorist views it as a single act. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **γενοῦ ἀνὴρ ἀγαθός.** — "Be a brave man."
- **παῦσαι λέγων.** — "Stop speaking."
- **μὴ φοβηθῇς τοὺς πολεμίους.** — "Do not fear the enemy."
- **ἀπόκριναι πρὸς τὴν ἐρώτησιν.** — "Answer the question."
- **λυθήτω ὁ δεσμώτης.** — "Let the prisoner be released."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Drama and dialogue.** Short imperatives of this kind carry the emotional register of tragedy and of Socratic conversation alike.
2. **Third person in decrees.** λυθήτω, ἀποδόσθω "let it be sold" is administrative Greek.
3. **Reading tip.** ‑σαι/‑οῦ = middle; ‑θητι = passive form. Then ask whether the verb has an active voice at all before deciding how to render it — a deponent takes an active sense.
