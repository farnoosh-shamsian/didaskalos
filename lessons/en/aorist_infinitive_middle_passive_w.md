# The Aorist Middle/Passive Infinitive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γίγνομαι, βούλομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Infinitive Is

An infinitive names the action; the aorist presents it as **one complete act**, without any reference to past time except in indirect statement.

In the aorist the two voices have separate forms, and they look nothing alike:

| Voice | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Middle | **λύσασθαι** | to free for oneself, to ransom |
| Passive | **λυθῆναι** | to be freed |

A row tagged "middle/passive" here is usually a **deponent** — a verb whose middle form carries active meaning — or a form the annotation left undecided.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Aorist Middle Infinitive: **λύσασθαι**

**aorist stem + ‑σα‑ + ‑σθαι**, with no augment:

| Verb | Infinitive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| λύω | **λύσασθαι** | to ransom |
| βουλεύω | **βουλεύσασθαι** | to deliberate |
| ἀποκρίνομαι | **ἀποκρίνασθαι** | to answer |

Thematic aorists take the thematic ending ‑έσθαι: **γενέσθαι** "to become", **λαβέσθαι** "to take hold of", **ἑλέσθαι** "to choose".

The accent is recessive in the sigmatic aorist (λύσασθαι) but fixed on the penult in the thematic aorist (γενέσθαι, λαβέσθαι).

### 2.2 The Aorist Passive Infinitive: **λυθῆναι**

**stem + ‑θῆναι**, always with a circumflex on the η:

| Verb | Infinitive | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| λύω | **λυθῆναι** | to be freed |
| γράφω | **γραφῆναι** | to be written |
| φοβέομαι | **φοβηθῆναι** | to be afraid (deponent) |

### 2.3 Where They Appear

| Construction | Example |
| --- | --- |
| After verbs of wishing, being able | βούλομαι λύσασθαι "I want to ransom" |
| Indirect statement | φησὶ λυθῆναι "he says he was freed" |
| Result clause (ὥστε) | ὥστε γενέσθαι φόβον "so that fear arose" |
| Articular infinitive | διὰ τὸ λυθῆναι "because of being freed" |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐλυσάμην and ἐλύθην are indicative; λύσασθαι and λυθῆναι are the infinitives. |
| **2. λύσασθαι vs. λῦσαι** | ‑σασθαι is the middle infinitive; λῦσαι alone is the *active* infinitive (and also the middle imperative). One syllable separates three forms. |
| **3. ‑θῆναι always has the circumflex** | λυθῆναι, γραφῆναι, ἀχθῆναι. The accent never recedes, whatever the length of the verb. |
| **4. Aspect, not time** | Except in indirect statement, βούλομαι λύσασθαι says nothing about when: the aorist marks the act as a single whole. |
| **5. Deponents take both shapes** | ἀποκρίνασθαι "to answer" (middle form) and φοβηθῆναι "to be afraid" (passive form) are both active in meaning. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **ἐβουλήθησαν τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους λύσασθαι.** — "They wished to ransom the prisoners."
- **οὐκ ἤθελε λυθῆναι ὑπὸ τῶν πολεμίων.** — "He did not want to be released by the enemy."
- **ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς βουλεύσασθαι περὶ τῆς εἰρήνης.** — "They decided to deliberate about the peace."
- **ὥστε φόβον γενέσθαι πᾶσιν.** — "So that fear came upon everyone."
- **λέγεται ἡ πόλις ἁλῶναι.** — "The city is said to have been captured."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Indirect statement** is the one place the aorist infinitive carries time: after φημί, λέγεται and similar, λυθῆναι reports "he was freed", not "he is freed".
2. **Prose formulas.** ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς + infinitive "they resolved to…" opens countless decrees; βουλεύσασθαι and ψηφίσασθαι are its usual completions.
3. **Reading tip.** ‑σασθαι: middle. ‑θῆναι: passive form. ‑έσθαι with no σ: thematic aorist middle.
