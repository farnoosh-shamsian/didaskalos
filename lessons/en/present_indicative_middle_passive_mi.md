# The Present Indicative Middle/Passive (μι-Verbs)

---

## 1. What the Present Indicative Middle/Passive Is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Present** | An action or state occurring now or habitually. |
| **Indicative** | The "statement" mood — for facts and ordinary questions. |
| **Middle** | The subject acts on or for itself: "I place for myself." |
| **Passive** | The subject **receives** the action; the agent can be added with ὑπό + genitive. |
| **(mi)** | This lesson concerns the athematic verbs: δίδωμι, τίθημι, ἵστημι, and middle‑only verbs such as δύναμαι and κεῖμαι. |

In the present, middle and passive share **the same endings**. What distinguishes the μι‑verbs is that these endings attach **directly to the stem, without a thematic vowel**.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 The Primary Middle Endings (Athematic)

| Person | Ending | Thematic (for comparison) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| 1st sg. | **‑μαι** | ‑ομαι |
| 2nd sg. | **‑σαι** | ‑ῃ / ‑ει (σ dropped) |
| 3rd sg. | **‑ται** | ‑εται |
| 1st pl. | **‑μεθα** | ‑όμεθα |
| 2nd pl. | **‑σθε** | ‑εσθε |
| 3rd pl. | **‑νται** | ‑ονται |

*Note the 2nd singular: the athematic verbs keep the σ of ‑σαι intact, where the thematic verbs lost it and contracted: τίθε‑σαι, δύνα‑σαι (contrast thematic λύῃ from λύε‑σαι).*

### 2.2 Construction: Short Present Stem + Ending

The middle/passive present uses the **short stem** (the same as in the active plural): τιθε‑, διδο‑, ἱστα‑.

### 2.3 Full Paradigms

**τίθημι "to place" → τίθεμαι "I place for myself / am placed":**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1st | **τίθεμαι** | **τιθέμεθα** |
| 2nd | **τίθεσαι** | **τίθεσθε** |
| 3rd | **τίθεται** | **τίθενται** |

**δίδωμι "to give" → δίδομαι:**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1st | **δίδομαι** | **διδόμεθα** |
| 2nd | **δίδοσαι** | **δίδοσθε** |
| 3rd | **δίδοται** | **δίδονται** |

**ἵστημι "to set up" → ἵσταμαι (middle: "I stand"):**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1st | **ἵσταμαι** | **ἱστάμεθα** |
| 2nd | **ἵστασαι** | **ἵστασθε** |
| 3rd | **ἵσταται** | **ἵστανται** |

### 2.4 High-Frequency Athematic Deponents

These middle‑only verbs conjugate exactly like the paradigms above and always **translate actively**:

| Verb | Meaning | 2nd/3rd sg. |
|------|------|------------------|
| **δύναμαι** | to be able | **δύνασαι / δύναται** |
| **ἐπίσταμαι** | to know, understand | **ἐπίστασαι / ἐπίσταται** |
| **κεῖμαι** | to lie, be situated | **κεῖσαι / κεῖται** |
| **κάθημαι** | to sit | **κάθησαι / κάθηται** |

## 3. Key Rules & Common Exceptions

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. No thematic vowel** | Endings attach straight to the stem. | δίδο‑ται, not *διδό‑ε‑ται |
| **2. The σ of ‑σαι survives** | The athematic verbs keep ‑σαι intact, where the thematic verbs lost the σ and contracted. | τίθεσαι vs. λύῃ |
| **3. Short stem throughout** | The long stem (τιθη‑, διδω‑) belongs to the active singular only. | τίθεμαι, δίδομαι |
| **4. Middle = passive in form** | Only context decides between "for oneself" and "is done to." | ἵσταται "he stands" or "is being set up" |
| **5. Deponents translate actively** | δύναμαι, ἐπίσταμαι, κεῖμαι have no active forms. | δύναται "he can" |
| **6. κεῖται often replaces the perfect passive of τίθημι** | An idiom of classical prose. | νόμος κεῖται "a law is laid down" |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

| Greek | Translation |
|----------------------------|-------------|
| **οὐ δύναμαι λέγειν.** | I am not able to speak. (deponent) |
| **τὰ δῶρα δίδοται τῷ θεῷ.** | The gifts are given to the god. (passive) |
| **ὁ νόμος κεῖται τοῖς πολίταις.** | The law is laid down for the citizens. |
| **ἐπίστασθε τὴν τέχνην.** | You (pl.) know the craft. (deponent) |

---

## 5. Usage & Context

1. **δύναμαι and ἐπίσταμαι are among the commonest verbs of Greek prose** — historians and orators use δύναται/δύνανται constantly for capability.
2. **Middle vs. passive** must be decided from context; with an agent phrase (ὑπό + genitive) read a passive, with a self‑interested subject read a middle.
3. **Recognizing the athematic 2nd singular** (‑σαι) instantly identifies a μι‑verb or athematic deponent in a text.
