# The Aorist Imperative Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, σῴζω, φοβέομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Imperative Is

A command that the subject **undergo** something: λύθητι "be freed", σώθητι "be saved", φοβήθητι "be afraid".

The aorist commands the action as one complete event. For an ongoing or repeated state, Greek uses the present middle/passive imperative (λύου) instead.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**aorist passive stem in ‑θη‑ + active-looking imperative endings**, with no augment.

| Person | Ending | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 sg | ‑θητι | **λύθητι** | be freed! |
| 3 sg | ‑θήτω | **λυθήτω** | let him be freed |
| 2 pl | ‑θητε | **λύθητε** | be freed! (pl.) |
| 3 pl | ‑θέντων | **λυθέντων** | let them be freed |

Later Greek also uses ‑θήτωσαν for the third plural.

### 2.2 Why ‑θητι and not ‑θηθι

The expected ending was ‑θι, which would have given *λύθηθι — two aspirates in successive syllables. Greek dissimilates the second to τ, exactly as in ἐτέθην for *ἐθέθην. Verbs with a **second** (θ-less) aorist passive keep the original ‑θι: γράφηθι "be written", φάνηθι "appear".

### 2.3 Common Verbs

| Verb | Aorist passive | Imperative 2 sg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| σῴζω "save" | ἐσώθην | **σώθητι** |
| φοβέομαι "fear" | ἐφοβήθην | **φοβήθητι** |
| πείθω "persuade" | ἐπείσθην | **πείσθητι** |
| ἐγείρω "wake" | ἠγέρθην | **ἐγέρθητι** |

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. No augment** | ἐλύθην is indicative; λύθητι is the imperative. |
| **2. Deponents give active meanings** | φοβήθητι means "be afraid", not "be feared"; πορεύθητι means "go". The ‑θη‑ shape does not guarantee passive sense. |
| **3. Prohibitions use μή + aorist subjunctive** | μὴ φοβηθῇς "do not be afraid" — there is no negative aorist imperative. |
| **4. Distinguish 2 sg from 2 pl** | λύθητι (singular) vs. λύθητε (plural). One letter separates them. |
| **5. η-aorist passives keep ‑θι** | φάνηθι, γράφηθι. If there is no θ in the stem, the ending has one. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **σώθητι, ὦ παῖ.** — "Save yourself, child."
- **μὴ φοβηθῇς, πίστευε μόνον.** — "Do not be afraid, only believe."
- **πείσθητι τοῖς νόμοις.** — "Be persuaded by the laws."
- **ἐγέρθητι καὶ ἆρον τὸν κράβαττόν σου.** — "Rise and take up your mat."
- **λυθήτω ὁ δεσμώτης.** — "Let the prisoner be freed."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Koine and drama.** Commands of this shape are frequent in the New Testament (ἐγέρθητι, καθαρίσθητι) and in tragedy, where a character is told to endure or be moved.
2. **Third person as a legal formula.** λυθήτω, ἀποδοθήτω "let it be given back" is the language of decrees.
3. **Reading tip.** ‑θητι, ‑θήτω, ‑θητε: the θη belongs to the passive stem, the endings are the ordinary imperative ones.
