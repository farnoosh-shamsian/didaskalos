# The Future Perfect Indicative Middle/Passive (ω-Verbs)
*(e.g., λύω, γράφω, μιμνῄσκομαι)*

---

## 1. What This Tense Is

The rarest tense in Greek, and the most specific: it says that a **state will be in force** at some future point. Not "it will be written" (that is the future passive γραφήσεται) but "**it will stand written**".

| Tense | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Future passive | γραφήσεται | it will be written (the act) |
| **Future perfect** | **γεγράψεται** | it will stand written (the state) |

The nuance is often one of certainty or finality: the thing will be settled, done, beyond recall.

---

## 2. Forms and Patterns

### 2.1 Formation

**reduplicated perfect middle/passive stem + ‑σ‑ + future middle endings**:

| Person | Form | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | **λελύσομαι** | I shall stand freed |
| 2 sg | **λελύσῃ** | you will stand freed |
| 3 sg | **λελύσεται** | it will stand freed |
| 1 pl | **λελυσόμεθα** | we shall stand freed |
| 2 pl | **λελύσεσθε** | you will stand freed |
| 3 pl | **λελύσονται** | they will stand freed |

### 2.2 The Forms You Will Actually Meet

Only a handful of verbs use this tense with any frequency, and they are worth knowing individually:

| Verb | Future perfect | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| μιμνῄσκομαι | **μεμνήσομαι** | I shall remember |
| καλέω | **κεκλήσομαι** | I shall be called |
| γράφω | **γεγράψεται** | it will stand written |
| θνῄσκω | **τεθνήξω / τεθνήξομαι** | I shall be dead |
| ἵστημι | **ἑστήξω** | I shall be standing |

μεμνήσομαι and κεκλήσομαι are common enough that many learners meet them without realizing which tense they are.

### 2.3 Periphrasis

Where no simple form exists, Greek uses the perfect participle with the future of εἰμί: **λελυμένος ἔσται** "he will have been freed".

---

## 3. Key Rules and Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Reduplication + σ** | λε‑λύ‑**σ**‑ομαι: the reduplication says perfect, the σ says future. |
| **2. State, not act** | γεγράψεται is about the document standing written, not about the writing happening. |
| **3. μεμνήσομαι means "I shall remember"** | Because μέμνημαι is "I remember", its future perfect is a plain future in force. |
| **4. Do not confuse with the future passive** | λυθήσεται "will be freed" vs. λελύσεται "will stand freed". The reduplication is the tell. |
| **5. Genuinely rare** | Outside the verbs listed above you will seldom meet it; the periphrasis is commoner. |

---

## 4. Example Sentences

- **μεμνήσομαι τῶν εὐεργεσιῶν.** — "I shall remember your kindnesses."
- **οὗτος προδότης κεκλήσεται.** — "This man will be called a traitor."
- **ταῦτα ἐν στήλῃ γεγράψεται.** — "This will stand written on a stele."
- **ἢν τοῦτο ποιήσῃς, ἅπαντα λελύσεται.** — "If you do this, everything will have been settled."

---

## 5. Usage and Context

1. **Emphatic finality.** Orators use the future perfect to say that a matter will be closed for good.
2. **Tragedy** likes τεθνήξω "I shall be dead" for its bleak completeness.
3. **Reading tip.** Reduplication plus σ plus a middle ending. If you see all three, you have found the rarest tense in the language.
